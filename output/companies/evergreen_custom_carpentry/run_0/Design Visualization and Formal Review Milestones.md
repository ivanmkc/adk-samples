# Design Visualization and Formal Review Milestones at Evergreen Custom Carpentry

Evergreen Custom Carpentry places significant emphasis on clear communication and client collaboration throughout the design process. A cornerstone of this approach involves sophisticated design visualization techniques coupled with structured formal review milestones, ensuring complete alignment before production commences.

## Advanced Design Visualization

To translate initial concepts into tangible plans, Evergreen utilizes industry-standard Computer-Aided Design (CAD) software. Precision 2D technical drawings, essential for workshop accuracy, are primarily developed using AutoCAD. For client visualization, the team employs SketchUp Pro, often enhanced with powerful rendering engines like V-Ray.

These tools enable the creation of photorealistic 3D renderings designed for high fidelity. Clients receive visualizations that accurately depict their chosen wood species (such as Appalachian Black Walnut or White Oak), the specific grain patterns from selected slabs, the exact finish sheen (matte, satin, etc.), selected hardware, and even simulated lighting conditions within the context of their actual space. This level of detail allows clients to fully understand the proposed design before committing to fabrication.

## Formal Review Stages

The design journey is punctuated by formal review milestones, typically structured around three key stages:

1.  **Initial Concept Review:** Occurs after preliminary hand sketches have been developed, allowing for discussion and refinement of the basic design direction.
2.  **Detailed Design Review:** Takes place upon completion of the primary CAD drawings and detailed 3D renderings, providing a comprehensive view of the proposed piece.
3.  **Pre-Production Review:** Serves as the final confirmation point before project-specific lumber and hardware are ordered, ensuring all details are finalized.

## The Review Process and Deliverables

Formal review meetings are dedicated sessions designed for thorough discussion and feedback. These meetings generally last between one to two hours and can be held either at the Burlington workshop or conducted conveniently via video conference. Attendees typically include the client(s), Silas Blackwood or the lead project designer, and often the client's architect or interior designer, fostering a collaborative environment.

Following the crucial Detailed Design Review milestone, clients receive a comprehensive digital package, usually delivered in PDF format. This package contains essential information, including:
*   Dimensioned floor plans
*   Detailed elevations of all relevant faces
*   Critical section views illustrating construction details
*   Multiple 3D perspective views

These digital design assets, along with high-resolution 3D renderings, are shared securely with clients, typically via email links or through a dedicated cloud-based project folder for easy access and review.

## Feedback, Revisions, and Formal Approval

Client feedback provided during formal review meetings is meticulously documented by the Evergreen team. Subsequent design revisions are carefully managed using version control within the CAD files. Any changes made are clearly communicated or highlighted in the next iteration presented to the client for approval.

Crucially, each formal review milestone requires explicit client sign-off. This typically involves a written approval, either through a physical or digital signature, on the presented drawings, specifications, or selection sheets. This sign-off formally authorizes Evergreen Custom Carpentry to proceed to the subsequent project phase.

The standard design fee structure accommodates a defined number of revision cycles (commonly one or two) following each major formal review meeting. Should clients request additional revisions beyond this agreed scope, further design charges may apply as outlined in the project contract.

## From Approved Design to Workshop Execution

The finalized and client-approved CAD drawings are not merely representational; they become the definitive guide for workshop operations. These precise digital blueprints directly inform the generation of accurate component cut lists and detailed assembly diagrams. Furthermore, they guide the craftspeople in laying out complex joinery and ensuring the precise installation of hardware, translating the approved design into a tangible, high-quality finished piece. This seamless transition from digital design to physical creation underscores the importance of the rigorous visualization and review process.
