# Fostering Expertise: Skill Sharing and Cross-Training at Evergreen Custom Carpentry

A core element of Evergreen Custom Carpentry's success lies in its deeply ingrained culture of continuous learning and collaborative skill development. This commitment manifests primarily through regular skill-sharing sessions and a dedicated approach to cross-training within the Burlington workshop team.

## Internal Skill-Share Meetings

To facilitate the exchange of knowledge and techniques, the workshop holds internal 'skill-share' meetings. These sessions are typically conducted bi-weekly, often utilizing extended lunch breaks on Fridays, and last approximately 60 to 90 minutes.

Leadership for these sessions frequently comes from senior craftspeople who demonstrate specialized techniques, such as advanced marquetry layout methods or the intricacies of sharpening complex molding plane irons. However, the platform is also open to apprentices, who occasionally present innovative solutions they have developed to overcome specific project challenges. This blend ensures a dynamic exchange of both established mastery and fresh perspectives.

The topics covered are diverse and directly relevant to ongoing work. A recent example from Q2 2024 involved a collaborative problem-solving session focused on achieving consistent clamping pressure for the curved lamination work essential for a custom arched doorway project. Attendance at these meetings is mandatory for apprentices, as the practical demonstrations often supplement and enrich the milestones outlined in their formal training logbooks.

## Cross-Training Initiatives

Beyond group sessions, Evergreen actively encourages experienced carpenters to broaden their expertise through cross-training. Each year, seasoned team members are prompted to identify at least one adjacent skill area they wish to develop. Progress in these areas is discussed during informal check-ins with founder Silas Blackwood.

This initiative focuses on practical capability rather than formal certification, fostering a flexible and organic approach to skill expansion. A specific instance involved a senior joinery specialist dedicating focused time over three weeks to learn advanced veneer matching and pressing techniques directly from the finishing department lead. This cross-pollination aimed to better integrate joinery processes with the requirements of veneered panels, enhancing overall project cohesion.

## Documenting and Supporting Learning

Valuable techniques or innovative jigs that emerge from skill-share sessions are often documented for future reference. This typically involves photographs and brief descriptions, which are compiled into an internal digital 'Workshop Techniques' guide. This resource is accessible to all staff via a shared network drive in the Burlington office.

Supporting this culture of continuous learning is a small, curated technical library maintained within the workshop. Accessible to all staff, it contains reference books by renowned woodworking authorities like Tage Frid and James Krenov, alongside subscriptions to key publications such as Fine Woodworking magazine.

## Tangible Results

The collaborative and knowledge-sharing environment yields significant practical benefits. For instance, the collective problem-solving approach integral to the skill-sharing culture was instrumental in developing a custom sequence for finishing and assembling a large, multi-component White Oak library unit. This collaborative effort ensured seamless grain matching across numerous pre-finished sections, achieving a critical aesthetic goal for the project.

In essence, the commitment to skill sharing and cross-training at Evergreen Custom Carpentry is not just a policy but a fundamental aspect of the workshop's identity, ensuring the team remains versatile, knowledgeable, and capable of tackling complex custom woodworking challenges with shared expertise.
