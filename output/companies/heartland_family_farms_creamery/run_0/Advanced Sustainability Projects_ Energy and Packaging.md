## Advancing Sustainability: Energy and Packaging Initiatives at Heartland Family Farms Creamery

Building upon its long-standing commitment to sustainable farming and transparent production, Heartland Family Farms Creamery is actively pursuing advanced projects aimed at further reducing its environmental footprint. Key initiatives focus on renewable energy generation through anaerobic digestion and transitioning to more sustainable packaging materials.

### Anaerobic Digester Project: Harnessing Energy from Manure

Heartland is currently undertaking a comprehensive feasibility study for the potential installation of an anaerobic digester system, targeting implementation around 2027. This forward-thinking project addresses several key sustainability goals:

*   **Manure Management:** The study is evaluating systems capable of processing the daily manure output from the farm's approximately 150-cow herd, estimated at 10,000 to 12,000 gallons per day.
*   **Renewable Energy Generation:** A primary objective is to capture methane emissions, a potent greenhouse gas, from the manure. This captured methane will be converted into biogas. The potential thermal energy generated from this biogas could meet over **75% of the creamery's significant hot water heating requirements**, which are crucial for pasteurization and cleaning processes.
*   **Enhanced Energy Independence:** The feasibility study includes assessing the economic viability of adding a **Combined Heat and Power (CHP)** unit to the digester system. This would allow the farm to generate electricity, supplementing the existing 50kW solar panel array installed in 2018 and further reducing reliance on grid power.
*   **Nutrient Recycling:** The nutrient-rich liquid byproduct of anaerobic digestion, known as digestate, is planned for integration into the farm's existing Nutrient Management Plan. This digestate offers a potentially more readily available source of fertilizer for the farm's 500+ acres of feed crops compared to the current practice of windrow composting manure.
*   **Funding and Investment:** Recognizing the significant capital investment required, estimated at **$1.5 to $2 million**, the study is actively exploring potential funding sources, including grant opportunities through programs like the USDA's Rural Energy for America Program (REAP).

### Sustainable Packaging Initiative: Reducing Environmental Impact

Parallel to the energy project, Heartland is driving a significant initiative to improve the sustainability of its product packaging, aiming to transition **50% of its packaging to sustainable materials by 2028**. This effort targets key packaging components across their product lines:

*   **Cheese and Butter Packaging:** The initiative specifically focuses on finding sustainable alternatives for the plastic film used to wrap the approximately **200,000 pounds of cheese** produced annually, and the foil/paper wrappers for the **5,000 pounds of butter** produced each year.
*   **Material Research and Testing:** Current research involves pilot testing plant-based **polylactic acid (PLA) films** for cheese wrapping. Evaluations are focused on critical performance factors like oxygen barrier properties and their impact on cheese shelf life, particularly for sensitive products like the 2-year Heritage Cheddar.
*   **Ice Cream Containers:** For the pint containers used for exclusive on-site ice cream sales, Heartland is exploring **Forest Stewardship Council (FSC) certified paperboard containers** featuring biodegradable linings. The goal is to replace the current standard coated paperboard containers with this more sustainable option by 2026.
*   **Academic Collaboration:** The initiative includes potential collaboration with the University of Wisconsin-Stout's renowned packaging program. This partnership would facilitate rigorous testing of new sustainable materials under real-world dairy processing conditions, assessing performance, compostability, and compatibility with existing packaging machinery.
*   **Consumer Education:** A key challenge being proactively addressed is the need for clear consumer education regarding the proper disposal of new packaging materials. This includes distinguishing between materials suitable for home composting versus those requiring specialized industrial composting facilities.

These ambitious projects underscore Heartland Family Farms Creamery's dedication to continuous improvement and long-term environmental stewardship, integrating innovative solutions into their farmstead dairy operation.
