# Dairy Herd Management and Milk Sourcing Strategy at Heartland Family Farms

The quality of artisanal dairy products begins at the source: the milk. Heartland Family Farms Creamery employs a meticulous strategy for managing its own dairy herd and sourcing supplemental milk, ensuring a consistent supply of high-quality milk perfectly suited for its cheese, butter, and ice cream production.

## Managing the Heartland Herd

Heartland's on-farm dairy operation is carefully managed to optimize both milk production and component quality, crucial for artisanal products.

### Herd Composition and Breeding

The farm maintains a herd of approximately 150 cows. The composition is strategically balanced, comprising roughly 60% Holstein cows, known for their high milk volume, and 40% Jersey cows, valued for their milk's high butterfat and protein content. This blend contributes richness and desirable characteristics to the final dairy products.

To continually improve herd quality, Heartland utilizes an Artificial Insemination (AI) breeding program. Sires are carefully selected based on genetic indexes that prioritize key traits: high butterfat percentage, Daughter Pregnancy Rate (DPR) for enhanced fertility, and positive udder health traits to minimize issues like mastitis.

### Feeding and Housing

Heartland leverages its 500+ acres for a rotational grazing system during Wisconsin's typical grazing season (May through October). The pastureland is divided into approximately 20 paddocks, averaging 25 acres each. Cows are moved to fresh pasture every 1-2 days, optimizing forage quality intake and allowing for effective pasture regrowth.

During the non-grazing months (typically November through April), the milking herd is housed comfortably in the farm's free-stall barn, originally constructed in 1988. Their diet consists of a Total Mixed Ration (TMR), primarily composed of corn silage and alfalfa haylage grown directly on the farm. This base ration is supplemented with a custom grain and mineral mix to meet their nutritional needs.

### Herd Health and Milk Quality

A comprehensive herd health program is maintained in partnership with the Green Valley Veterinary Clinic. This includes routine vaccinations, regular hoof trimming schedules, and proactive mastitis detection protocols. These efforts contribute significantly to maintaining excellent udder health and consistently achieving a low average somatic cell count (SCC) below 150,000 cells/mL, a key indicator of milk quality.

### Calf Rearing

Raising healthy replacement heifers is vital for the future of the herd. Newborn calves receive essential colostrum within two hours of birth. They are then raised in individual calf hutches for about 8 weeks, allowing for close health monitoring and individualized care. Weaning from milk replacer typically occurs around 10 weeks of age.

### Milk Handling on the Farm

Immediately after milking, the milk harvested from the Heartland herd is rapidly cooled using a high-efficiency plate cooler. This process brings the milk temperature down to below 40°F (4°C) within two hours, preserving its freshness and quality before it is stored in a 6,000-gallon insulated bulk tank located adjacent to the milking parlor.

## Sourcing Supplemental Milk

To meet production demands, Heartland supplements its own milk supply by partnering with local farms.

### Partner Farm Network

Heartland collaborates with five local, family-operated dairy farms, all situated within a 20-mile radius of the creamery. This proximity ensures milk freshness. These partner farms typically milk between 40 and 75 cows each, predominantly Holstein or Holstein-cross breeds.

### Collection and Standards

Milk from these partner farms is collected daily using Heartland's dedicated 3,000-gallon refrigerated tanker truck. Upon pickup at each farm, the milk is tested for antibiotics and temperature before being pooled with other milk. Critically, all partner farms contractually adhere to Heartland's stringent standards for animal welfare and guarantee the production of rBST-free milk.

## Ensuring Overall Milk Richness

Across both its own herd and its partner farms, Heartland aims for a target butterfat percentage exceeding 4.2% in the combined milk supply arriving at the creamery. This high butterfat level is essential for achieving the desired richness and texture in its artisanal butter and cheese products.

Through careful herd management, strategic breeding, high health standards, and close partnerships with neighboring farms, Heartland Family Farms Creamery ensures a consistent supply of premium milk, forming the foundation of its award-winning dairy products.
