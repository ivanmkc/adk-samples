# Founding and Family Legacy of Heartland Family Farms Creamery

## Humble Beginnings in Green Valley County

The story of Heartland Family Farms Creamery began in 1948, rooted in the post-war aspirations of first-generation farmers Jacob and Esther Miller. Returning from service in World War II, Jacob Miller utilized his personal savings along with a small agricultural loan to purchase the initial 80 acres of farmland in Green Valley County, Wisconsin. The original Miller farm started modestly, home to just 12 Holstein cows. In these early years, the focus was primarily on supplying fresh milk to the local community.

## Early Growth and Diversification

Driven by a desire to build something lasting, Jacob and Esther took their first step into dairy processing in 1957. They constructed a small, rudimentary creamery on the farm premises, allowing them to produce butter and basic cheddar cheese. This marked a significant shift from solely supplying fluid milk, laying the groundwork for the creamery operations that would define their future.

## Generational Transitions and Expansion

The farm and its burgeoning creamery became a true family endeavor. In 1975, leadership transitioned to the second generation as Jacob and Esther's son, David Miller, took over the farm's management. Recognizing the potential for growth and diversification, David spearheaded the first major expansion in the early 1980s. This pivotal move involved constructing a dedicated cheese-making facility, enabling the farm to significantly broaden its product offerings beyond butter and basic cheddar.

Over the subsequent decades, the farm itself saw gradual expansion, growing from its initial 80 acres to its current size of over 500 acres, providing ample space for pasture and growing feed for the herd.

## Continuing the Legacy: The Third Generation

Today, Heartland Family Farms Creamery continues under the stewardship of the third generation of the Miller family. Jacob's granddaughter, Sarah Miller, now serves as CEO, guiding the business while upholding the values established over 70 years ago. The family's connection to its roots remains strong; Esther Miller's original handwritten recipe book, filled with notes on butter churning and farmhouse cheddar, is carefully preserved in the family archives. These historical recipes occasionally serve as inspiration for unique, seasonal product variations, blending tradition with innovation.

The Miller family's commitment extends beyond the farm gate, dating back to the 1950s when they played a role in organizing the first Green Valley County Dairy Fair, demonstrating a long-standing dedication to their local community. This deep history forms the bedrock of Heartland Family Farms Creamery, shaping its identity as a multi-generational enterprise committed to quality and tradition.
