# Expansion Over Decades: Heartland's Farm and Facility Growth

Heartland Family Farms Creamery, established in 1948 on a modest 80 acres, has undergone significant physical expansion over its 70+ year history. This growth reflects a multi-generational commitment to developing the farm's capacity for both dairy farming and artisanal cheese production.

## Early Land Acquisition

The first notable expansion of the farm's footprint occurred gradually between 1965 and 1970. During this period, as neighboring farmers retired, the Miller family acquired two smaller, adjacent land parcels. This incremental growth increased the total farm size from the original 80 acres to 120 acres, providing more space for pasture and feed cultivation.

## Major Land Expansion Under David Miller

A more substantial period of land acquisition took place under the management of second-generation leader, David Miller. Between 1985 and 1995, Heartland purchased three adjacent farms, adding approximately 380 acres to the operation. This significant expansion brought the total farm size to its current scale of over 500 acres, enabling a larger herd and increased self-sufficiency in feed production.

## Evolution of Creamery Facilities

The original creamery, built by founder Jacob Miller in 1957, was a simple 20x30 foot wooden structure. Initially used for producing butter and basic cheddar, this building served its purpose for 25 years.

In 1982, recognizing the need for increased capacity and diversification, David Miller spearheaded the construction of a new, dedicated cheese-making facility. This purpose-built 5,000 square foot structure represented a major leap forward. It housed significantly larger equipment, including a 500-gallon pasteurizer and two 1,000-gallon cheese vats. This investment enabled Heartland to expand its cheddar production significantly and diversify into making Colby and Monterey Jack cheeses. Following the opening of the 1982 facility, the original 1957 wooden creamery was decommissioned for dairy processing and subsequently converted into a dedicated workshop for farm equipment repair and maintenance.

## Developing Supporting Infrastructure

The expansion under David Miller necessitated upgrades to supporting farm infrastructure:

*   **Housing:** To accommodate the growing herd size, a new free-stall barn with capacity for 100 cows was constructed in 1988, replacing older, smaller tie-stall facilities and improving animal comfort.
*   **Feed Storage:** As acreage expanded in the late 1980s, two concrete stave silos were erected near the main barn complex to increase storage capacity for corn silage harvested from the farm's land.
*   **Milking:** A significant upgrade occurred in 1998 with the installation of a modern double-8 herringbone milking parlor, enhancing efficiency and cow comfort for the herd, which numbered around 150 cows at the time.
*   **Cheese Aging:** A dedicated cheese aging cellar was added in 1995. This 1,500 square foot, partially subterranean structure, built with reinforced concrete, was specifically designed to maintain the optimal 50-55°F temperature and high humidity crucial for aging high-quality cheddars.

## Enhancing the Visitor Experience

Prior to 2005, direct sales to consumers occurred primarily through a small, informal counter area within the main 1982 cheese-making facility. To enhance the customer experience and further diversify revenue streams, a dedicated visitor center was constructed in 2005. This 4,000 square foot building, attached to the main creamery, incorporates the retail store, a dedicated tasting room, public restrooms, and large observation windows overlooking the cheese-making floor, offering a more formal and engaging experience for the public.

Through strategic land purchases and timely investments in modern facilities, Heartland Family Farms Creamery has consistently expanded its physical operations over the decades, laying the groundwork for its continued success as a leading artisanal cheese producer.
