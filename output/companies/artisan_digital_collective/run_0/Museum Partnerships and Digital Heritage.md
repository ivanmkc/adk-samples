# Artisan Digital Collective: Museum Partnerships and Digital Heritage

Artisan Digital Collective (ADC) is actively engaging with cultural institutions to explore the frontiers of digital heritage, focusing on digitization, virtual experiences, education, and preservation. These collaborations leverage ADC's technological expertise to bring museum collections into immersive digital environments.

## Pilot Project with Centre Pompidou

A significant initiative involves a pilot project with the renowned Centre Pompidou in Paris. This collaboration centers on the high-fidelity digitization of approximately 15 key works from the museum's New Media collection. Notable pieces by artists such as Nam June Paik and Bill Viola are included. The digitization process utilizes advanced 3D scanning and photogrammetry techniques, managed by a joint technical team from ADC and the Pompidou.

Following the digitization, ADC and the Centre Pompidou plan to launch a joint virtual exhibition. This exhibition will be hosted within a dedicated, co-branded wing of ADC's virtual reality platform, built using Unreal Engine 5. Discussions are ongoing regarding access models, with the potential for free access tiers specifically for museum members. The targeted launch for this virtual exhibition is late 2025.

The initial funding for this pilot project is structured as a joint investment. ADC contributes significant technological resources and development time, valued at approximately €500,000. This is complemented by grant funding secured by the Centre Pompidou, specifically aimed at digital innovation and outreach initiatives.

## Educational Applications and Interactive Features

Beyond exhibitions, ADC is venturing into educational applications of its technology. The collective is developing its first educational VR module prototype, titled 'Decoding Digital Art.' This module is being created in consultation with curators, potentially including advisors from institutions like the Whitney Museum of American Art. It targets university-level art history students and leverages features like the VR 'Inspection Mode' for in-depth analysis of digitized artworks.

These educational VR modules aim to be highly interactive. Plans include sessions where student avatars can participate in live Q&A sessions with virtual representations of museum curators. This interaction could utilize pre-recorded volumetric video or live avatar interactions within the ADC VR space, offering new modes of engagement with museum expertise.

## Technical Standards, Security, and Intellectual Property

ADC employs specific technical standards in its museum digitization partnerships. For 2D assets, standards compatible with the International Image Interoperability Framework (IIIF) are used where feasible, promoting potential cross-platform compatibility. However, the immersive 3D and VR display remains optimized for ADC's proprietary Unreal Engine 5 environment.

Data security is paramount in these collaborations. Digitized museum assets acquired through partnerships are stored on segregated, encrypted servers located within ADC's Frankfurt data center. Strict data security protocols are negotiated with each institution, ensuring compliance with regulations such as GDPR, particularly relevant for European partners like the Centre Pompidou.

Intellectual property is managed through carefully negotiated licensing agreements. Typically, these grant ADC exclusive rights to display the digitized works within its VR and AR platforms for a defined period, often ranging from 5 to 7 years. These agreements frequently incorporate revenue-sharing clauses related to ticketed virtual events or premium educational content derived from the partnership.

## AI Integration and Future Research

ADC is also proposing the integration of its proprietary AI tools to benefit museum partners. Components of its 'Project Veracity' AI tool are offered as a value-added service to assist museums in analyzing the condition and verifying authenticity markers of their existing digital-born or digitized time-based media artworks.

Looking ahead, ADC is exploring joint research initiatives that extend beyond exhibition and digitization. Collaborations with museum conservation departments are being considered, focusing on developing best practices for the long-term preservation of complex digital artworks. This includes investigating potential migration strategies for digital art represented within the ADC platform, addressing critical challenges in the field of digital heritage conservation.
