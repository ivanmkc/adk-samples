# Riverbend BookNook & Café: Sourcing New and Used Inventory

Riverbend BookNook & Café maintains a carefully curated selection of both new and used books, with a distinct emphasis on Southern literature and local authors. This unique inventory mix is achieved through a multi-pronged sourcing strategy that balances efficiency for mainstream titles with dedicated efforts to acquire regional works and high-quality used books.

## Sourcing New Books

Riverbend employs a dual approach for acquiring new inventory:

1.  **Primary Distributor:** Approximately 70% of new book inventory is sourced through Ingram Content Group's iPage platform. This partnership leverages Ingram's extensive distribution network, allowing for streamlined ordering and efficient fulfillment of a wide range of mainstream titles and backlist books.
2.  **Direct Publisher Relationships:** To bolster its focus on regional literature, store managers actively cultivate direct purchasing relationships with Southern publishers. This includes working with smaller independent presses like Hub City Press (Spartanburg, SC) and John F. Blair, Publisher (Winston-Salem, NC), as well as Southern university presses. Managers often connect with representatives from these presses at industry events such as the Southern Independent Booksellers Alliance (SIBA) Discovery Show. Orders placed directly with these smaller presses typically involve smaller, less frequent shipments compared to Ingram, often arriving via UPS Ground or USPS Media Mail. Managers may consolidate orders quarterly to meet potential minimum purchase requirements, such as a $100 minimum for free shipping offered by some presses. This direct engagement ensures access to unique regional titles that align with Riverbend's niche focus.

## Acquiring Used Books

Used books form a significant part of Riverbend's appeal and are primarily acquired through two main channels:

1.  **Customer Trade-In Program:** This is the cornerstone of used book acquisition. Customers can bring in their gently used books for evaluation.
    *   **Store Credit:** Accepted books earn store credit, typically calculated at 40% of Riverbend's intended resale price (which is usually set at 50-60% of the original list price). This credit is electronically tracked within the customer's profile in the BookManager Point-of-Sale (POS) system and notably, does not expire, encouraging repeat visits.
    *   **Processing:** The BookManager POS system features a dedicated module for handling trade-ins. Staff use it to assess book condition against predefined criteria, calculate the store credit, and add the book to inventory with a unique used-item SKU.
    *   **Condition Standards:** Quality is maintained through strict condition standards. Books are commonly rejected for excessive highlighting or underlining (affecting over 10% of pages), strong smoke or mildew odors detected during inspection, broken spines, or detached covers.
    *   **Transaction Volume:** While there isn't a strict limit on the number of books brought in at once, transactions involving more than 25 books often require manager approval or scheduling an appointment to ensure adequate staff time for proper assessment.
    *   **Staff Training:** The importance of this process is underscored by dedicated training. The 'Riverbend Way' orientation includes a 45-minute practical session on used book evaluation, using examples and role-playing customer interactions.
2.  **Manager Purchases (Estate Sales, etc.):** Store managers supplement trade-ins by selectively purchasing used books from other sources. They identify promising estate sales through local newspaper listings, specialized websites (like EstateSales.net), and relationships with local antique dealers. When purchasing from these sources, managers prioritize collectible editions, local history titles, and out-of-print Southern fiction, further enhancing the store's unique offerings.

## Local Author Consignment Program

Riverbend actively supports local writers through a consignment program. Authors residing within a 50-mile radius of a store can submit a formal application, which must include a physical copy of their book for review. If accepted, the books are typically displayed for a 90-day period. Unsold books are generally returned to the author after this period, unless a mutual agreement is made to extend the consignment term. This program provides valuable visibility for local authors within their communities.

In summary, Riverbend's sourcing strategy is a dynamic blend of large-scale distribution for efficiency, direct relationships to foster regional connections, a robust trade-in program to engage customers and acquire affordable inventory, and targeted purchasing to secure unique finds. This comprehensive approach is essential to maintaining the diverse and community-focused collection that defines the Riverbend BookNook & Café experience.
