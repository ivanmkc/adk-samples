# Philosophy in Practice: The Original Riverbend BookNook & Café Design

When Eleanor Ainsworth, a former librarian, and her husband Thomas, an experienced barista, opened the first Riverbend BookNook & Café in a historic Savannah, Georgia building in the spring of 2003, they weren't just opening a store; they were launching an experiment in community building. Inspired by sociologist Ray Oldenburg's concept of the "third place" and their own observations of successful independent shops, their vision was meticulously translated into the physical design and atmosphere of the flagship location. The original store layout and features were a direct reflection of their core philosophies, particularly the idea of "intentional inefficiency" designed to encourage lingering and discovery.

## Layout Designed for Browsing

Departing from typical retail models focused on quick transactions, the original Savannah store implemented a layout embodying "intentional inefficiency." The main checkout counter was deliberately placed towards the rear of the space. This encouraged customers to walk deeper into the store, passing through various sections and increasing the likelihood of browsing before making a purchase. Complementing this, and reflecting Eleanor's library background, the layout featured wide, accessible aisles, inviting comfortable exploration rather than hurried navigation.

## Cultivating a Comfortable Atmosphere

Creating a welcoming "third place" atmosphere was paramount. Eight distinct upholstered armchairs, primarily sourced from local Savannah antique shops, were strategically clustered in pairs near windows and quieter corners. This provided comfortable nooks for reading and quiet conversation, distinct from standard retail seating.

The sensory environment was carefully controlled. Harsh fluorescent lights, common in retail, were avoided. Instead, the store utilized primarily warm-toned, low-wattage incandescent track lighting, supplemented by table lamps in the seating areas to create a softer, more inviting glow. The auditory environment was equally considered. The initial music policy, established by the Ainsworths, strictly limited the playlist to instrumental jazz and classical selections. Crucially, the volume was consistently kept below 65 decibels to ensure it remained background ambiance, facilitating conversation and reading rather than overwhelming the space.

## Celebrating the Local

A commitment to the local community was evident from the moment one entered. Mandated by Eleanor's initial vision, a prominent "Local Voices" section occupied a custom-built, 10-foot-wide wooden shelving unit positioned immediately to the right of the main entrance, ensuring high visibility for Savannah-area authors and history.

This local focus extended to the café. Thomas Ainsworth established direct relationships with Georgia-based micro-roasters. One of the three initial suppliers selected in 2003 was Coastal Plains Coffee Roasters from Statesboro, chosen specifically for its appealing medium-roast profile, offering customers a unique local taste.

## Heritage, Aesthetics, and Operations

The renovation of the historic building between 2002 and 2003 respected its past. Original exposed brickwork discovered on one interior wall was intentionally preserved and highlighted, becoming a key design feature that grounded the store in its Savannah heritage.

Reflecting Eleanor's professional background and adding a traditional bookish aesthetic, the original store layout included two dedicated library-style rolling ladders on tracks. These provided practical access to higher shelves in the taller non-fiction sections while enhancing the store's character. Even the exterior signage, installed in spring 2003, was thoughtfully designed, featuring the name "Riverbend BookNook & Café" alongside a simple, stylized river bend graphic created by a Savannah College of Art and Design student.

The philosophy extended to the first team members. The initial cohort of five part-time employees (three for the bookstore, two baristas) underwent an intensive four-day training personally led by Eleanor and Thomas. This training covered not only practical skills like SCA-standard coffee preparation and book genres but also deeply ingrained the "third place" philosophy that was central to Riverbend's identity.

From the layout and lighting to the seating and sounds, every element of the original Riverbend BookNook & Café in Savannah was a deliberate choice, carefully orchestrated by the Ainsworths to bring their vision of a community-focused "third place" to life.
