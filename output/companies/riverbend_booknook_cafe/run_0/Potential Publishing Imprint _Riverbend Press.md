# Riverbend Press: Exploring a Potential Publishing Imprint

Riverbend BookNook & Café is currently evaluating the possibility of launching its own publishing imprint, tentatively named 'Riverbend Press'. A comprehensive feasibility study is underway, spearheaded by co-founder Eleanor Ainsworth. This study incorporates valuable input from two senior store managers, noted for their strong backgrounds in curating Southern literature, as well as insights from a contracted publishing consultant who specializes in small press startups.

## Defining the Niche: Southern Voices and Regional Histories

If established, Riverbend Press aims to carve out a distinct niche within the publishing landscape. The primary focus would be on debut Southern novels, specifically targeting contemporary literary fiction and narrative non-fiction that delve into modern Southern identity. This initiative seeks to discover and amplify voices often overlooked by larger New York publishing houses.

Complementing the fiction focus, the imprint also plans to publish regional history monographs. The emphasis here will be on accessible, narrative-driven works exploring under-represented local histories within the Southeastern communities served by Riverbend stores, deliberately steering clear of purely academic treatises.

## Evaluating Feasibility: Costs, Sales, and Staffing

The feasibility study is meticulously examining key operational and financial metrics. Projected production costs per title are estimated to fall between $8,000 and $12,000 for initial print runs ranging from 1,000 to 1,500 copies. The study is also analyzing potential sales velocity, comparing sales through Riverbend's own network of stores against the potential reach of wider distribution channels. Furthermore, the required staffing overhead for the imprint is being carefully assessed.

A critical milestone within this evaluation process is the completion of a detailed 5-year financial projection model for Riverbend Press. This model is scheduled for internal review by Eleanor Ainsworth and the company's CFO by the first quarter of 2025.

## Potential Operations and Distribution

Should the decision be made to proceed, Riverbend Press has outlined initial operational plans. Manuscript submissions would likely be managed through an online system, such as Submittable. Initially, the imprint anticipates soliciting manuscripts primarily through agented submissions and targeted scouting efforts, leveraging recommendations from knowledgeable Riverbend store managers.

Eleanor Ainsworth is projected to serve as the initial Editor-in-Chief. Contingent on the imprint's launch and early performance, there is potential to hire one part-time Assistant Editor within the first year of operation.

Distribution strategies under evaluation prioritize leveraging Riverbend's existing store network. Projections suggest that 30-40% of initial sales could come from guaranteed placement and direct marketing within Riverbend locations. This would be supplemented by regional wholesale partnerships and potential inclusion in the Ingram catalogue for broader reach. The study also includes modeling the impact of featuring one Riverbend Press title annually within the planned 'Riverbend Reads & Roasts' subscription box, analyzing its potential effect on both imprint sales and subscriber engagement.

## The Path Forward: Decision Timeline

The ultimate go/no-go decision for launching Riverbend Press is anticipated by mid-2025. This decision hinges significantly on two key factors: securing favorable distribution terms, potentially through a major distributor like Ingram Publisher Services, and projecting profitability for the imprint within a three-year timeframe following its launch.
