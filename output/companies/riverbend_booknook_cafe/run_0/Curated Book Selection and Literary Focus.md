# Cultivating a Literary Identity: Riverbend's Curated Book Selection

Riverbend BookNook & Café distinguishes itself not only through its welcoming 'third place' atmosphere but also through a carefully curated book selection that emphasizes Southern literature and local authors. This intentional focus is a cornerstone of the brand's identity and its commitment to the communities it serves.

## Defining the Southern Niche

To guide its curation efforts, Riverbend employs a specific definition of "Southern literature." This includes:
*   Works by authors who were born in or have resided long-term within the Southeastern United States.
*   Books where the primary setting is located within the region.
*   Non-fiction titles centered on the history, culture, or environment of the South.

This definition allows for a nuanced collection that reflects the diverse literary landscape of the region.

## Inventory Strategy: Balancing Focus and Breadth

Riverbend strategically balances its niche focus with the need to cater to a wide range of reader interests. The company aims for Southern literature and local author titles (combining both new and used books) to represent a significant portion of its stock, targeting approximately 20-25% of the total fiction inventory and 15% of the overall book collection across its locations.

However, this specialization does not come at the expense of broader appeal. Riverbend mandates maintaining robust, well-stocked sections for major genres. At least 60% of the total inventory is dedicated to popular categories like Mystery/Thriller, Science Fiction/Fantasy, Contemporary Fiction, History, and Children's Literature, ensuring a comprehensive selection for all customers.

## Sourcing: Mainstream Efficiency and Regional Connections

Acquiring inventory involves a dual approach. For efficient ordering of mainstream new titles, Riverbend utilizes the Ingram Content Group. Simultaneously, store managers are actively encouraged to cultivate direct ordering relationships with Southern university presses, such as UNC Press and LSU Press, as well as independent regional publishers. This direct engagement allows Riverbend to stock unique titles and support the regional publishing ecosystem.

## Championing Local Voices

Supporting local authors is a key component of Riverbend's mission. Most locations offer consignment terms for self-published books by authors residing within a 50-mile radius. The typical arrangement involves a 60/40 sales split (favoring the author) and guarantees the book a display period of at least 90 days in the dedicated "Local Voices" section, often prominently placed.

## The Used Book Program

The used book section complements the new inventory and relies heavily on a customer trade-in program. To maintain quality, specific condition standards are enforced: books must have intact spines and covers, be free from water damage, contain minimal underlining or highlighting, and not possess excessive odors like smoke or mildew. Accepted trade-ins are typically priced for resale at 50-60% of the original publisher's list price, though managers have discretion to adjust pricing based on condition, edition rarity, and current stock levels.

## Internal Reinforcement and Staff Engagement

Riverbend actively cultivates its literary focus internally. Co-founder Eleanor Ainsworth distributes a quarterly "Southern Spotlight" digital newsletter to all store managers. This newsletter highlights 5-10 noteworthy Southern titles (both upcoming releases and backlist gems) and suggests related event ideas.

Furthermore, the mandatory "Riverbend Way" orientation program includes a dedicated 90-minute module titled "Knowing Our Niche." This training covers key figures in Southern literary history (like Faulkner, Welty, and Hurston) and equips staff with techniques for identifying and promoting local authors effectively.

This focus is also visible on the shop floor through the chain-wide "Riverbend Recommends" program. Each store is required to maintain a prominent shelf display featuring at least six "Staff Picks" each month, with a guideline stipulating that at least half of these selections should be Southern or local titles.

Through this multi-faceted approach—defining its niche, strategically sourcing inventory, supporting local talent, managing a quality used book program, and reinforcing its focus through internal training and promotion—Riverbend BookNook & Café crafts a unique literary identity that resonates with its regional roots and fosters a vibrant reading community.
