# Founding Philosophy and the 'Third Place' Concept at Riverbend

The genesis of Riverbend BookNook & Café lies in the shared vision of its founders, Eleanor and Thomas Ainsworth, and their desire to create more than just a retail space. Their initial concept was sparked during a trip through the Pacific Northwest in the late 1990s. They were deeply impressed by independent shops that successfully merged a vibrant café culture with a welcoming retail environment, planting the seed for what Riverbend would become.

This vision was significantly shaped by the founders' distinct professional backgrounds. Eleanor Ainsworth drew upon her 15 years of experience as a public librarian in Atlanta. This expertise was crucial in meticulously planning Riverbend's initial collection development strategy and formulating community outreach programs designed to engage local residents from the outset. Complementing this, Thomas Ainsworth brought 7 years of experience as a lead barista and trainer for a specialty coffee chain, also in Atlanta. His background directly informed Riverbend's unwavering commitment to high-quality espresso preparation and rigorous staff training, establishing a standard for the café component from day one.

Central to the Ainsworths' plan was the sociological concept of the "third place." They explicitly referenced Ray Oldenburg's influential 1989 book, 'The Great Good Place,' in their original business plan. Their stated intention was to establish Riverbend as a vital 'third place' for Savannah – a welcoming, accessible space separate from home (the first place) and work (the second place) where community could flourish.

The name 'Riverbend' itself was carefully chosen by Eleanor and Thomas. It aimed to evoke a sense of calm and natural gathering, much like a town situated on the bend of a river, while also nodding to the flagship store's location near the Savannah River.

Following nearly two years of dedicated planning and renovation work, the first Riverbend BookNook & Café opened its doors in Savannah, Georgia, in the spring of 2003. The physical design and operational policies were deliberately crafted to foster the intended 'third place' atmosphere. The original store featured varied seating options, including comfortable armchairs, small tables for individuals or pairs, and larger communal tables to encourage interaction. A strict policy against overly loud music was enforced to maintain a relaxed and conducive environment for reading, conversation, and contemplation. Furthermore, the founders embraced a philosophy of 'intentional inefficiency' in the store's layout. This approach encouraged browsing and lingering, deliberately moving away from the quick-transaction model favored by larger retail chains.

This founding philosophy manifested in specific features from the beginning. Eleanor's vision mandated a prominent 'Local Voices' section situated near the entrance, dedicated to showcasing Savannah-area authors and local history, with content refreshed monthly. On the café side, Thomas established direct relationships with three specific Georgia-based micro-roasters for the initial coffee supply. This ensured unique, high-quality flavor profiles that weren't readily available elsewhere, further distinguishing Riverbend's offerings and reinforcing its commitment to quality and local connections.
