# Confluence Urban Systems: Mechanisms for Citizen Engagement

A core principle guiding Confluence Urban Systems is the integration of citizen perspectives into the design and operation of smart city solutions. To achieve this, the company employs several key mechanisms designed to foster communication, gather feedback, and ensure community needs are addressed throughout project lifecycles.

## Citizen Advisory Panels (CAPs)

Citizen Advisory Panels serve as a crucial forum for direct community input on Confluence projects. Recruitment for these panels prioritizes reflecting the diversity of the project area. A public application process, promoted through city channels and community organizations, aims to select members representing varied demographics in terms of age, ethnicity, income, and geographic location within the affected area.

Members typically serve a two-year term, with appointments staggered to maintain continuity and institutional knowledge within the panel. CAPs generally meet quarterly, and to ensure transparency, meeting minutes are usually made publicly available on the respective city's project website within two weeks of each meeting.

The feedback provided by CAPs has tangible impacts on project implementation. For example, in an Austin traffic optimization project, input from the local CAP highlighted the needs of shift workers. This led directly to adjustments in signal timing algorithms during specific off-peak hours (10 PM - 5 AM), a change implemented in the third quarter of 2021.

## 'My[CityName] Connect' Mobile Application

Confluence develops customizable mobile applications, often branded 'My[CityName] Connect', to provide residents with direct access to city services and a channel for reporting issues. When users submit service requests via the app, they typically receive an automated acknowledgment within one hour. Requests are then routed to the relevant city department.

Resolution times are guided by Service Level Agreements (SLAs) established with the municipality, varying by the type of issue reported (e.g., 48 hours for pothole reports, 72 hours for non-hazardous code violations).

This platform also serves as a vital feedback mechanism influencing city operations. In Vancouver, feedback gathered through the app regarding accessibility concerns prompted Confluence to add a specific reporting category for sidewalk obstructions in the first quarter of 2023. Subsequent reports submitted under this category directly influenced the prioritization schedule of the city's Public Works department.

Recognizing the importance of equitable access, Confluence pilots multilingual support within the app interface, starting with Spanish, as first implemented during the Phoenix waste management project expansion in 2022. Future development plans aim to broaden the app's functionality beyond service requests, potentially including optional modules for localized polling on proposed neighborhood improvements and integration with city event calendars.

## Digital Inclusion Initiatives

To ensure residents can effectively utilize digital engagement tools, Confluence actively partners with local organizations, such as the Austin Public Library system, to co-deliver Digital Inclusion Workshops. Using a curriculum developed in-house by Confluence's community engagement team, these workshops typically cover essential skills like basic smartphone operation, navigating the 'My[CityName] Connect' app, understanding open data portals (including interpreting metrics like Air Quality Index readings), and best practices for online privacy.

## Integration and Reporting

Feedback gathered through both CAPs and the 'My[CityName] Connect' app is not merely collected; it is systematically integrated into project oversight. This feedback serves as a key input during the periodic reviews stipulated by the 'Adaptive Management Clauses' often included in Confluence's Public-Private Partnership (PPP) agreements. These clauses allow for adjustments to project Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) or service delivery strategies, typically every 3-5 years, based on performance data and community input.

Furthermore, Confluence provides quarterly reports to its partner cities, summarizing key citizen engagement metrics. These reports include data such as active user counts for the 'My[CityName] Connect' app, the volume and categorization of 311 requests submitted via the app, CAP meeting attendance rates, and anonymized thematic summaries of the feedback received across all channels. To further enhance engagement equity, Confluence undertakes targeted CAP recruitment drives in underserved neighborhoods, partnering with community leaders, as demonstrated during the 2022 Phoenix project expansion.

Through these interconnected mechanisms – direct dialogue via CAPs, accessible digital tools like the 'My[CityName] Connect' app, supportive digital literacy programs, and formal integration into project management – Confluence Urban Systems strives to embed citizen voices at the heart of its smart city initiatives.
