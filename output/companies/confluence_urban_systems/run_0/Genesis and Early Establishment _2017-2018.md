# Genesis and Early Establishment (2017-2018)

The foundational period for Confluence Urban Systems spanned from late 2017 through 2018, marked by the transition from an academic concept to a formally established and funded entity poised for initial operations.

## Conceptual Origins and Early Guidance

The intellectual groundwork for Confluence Urban Systems was laid in 2017 with a foundational research paper co-authored by future founders Dr. Marcus Thorne and Anya Sharma. This paper, which conceptualized the company's core ideas around integrating sensor data for urban optimization, gained initial visibility when it was presented during a technical session at the IEEE Smart Cities Conference held in Chicago in September 2017.

During this critical formation phase in late 2017 and early 2018, the nascent company benefited from the informal guidance of Dr. Elena Petrova. An expert in urban data ethics and a former colleague of Dr. Thorne's from MIT, Dr. Petrova played a key advisory role in helping shape the company's early data governance principles.

## Incorporation and Initial Operations

Building on the conceptual framework and initial momentum, Confluence Urban Systems was officially incorporated as a Delaware C-Corporation in February 2018. This legal step followed the commitment of initial seed funding.

Before securing dedicated headquarters, the company operated out of a shared workspace within the prominent 1871 tech incubator, located in Chicago's Merchandise Mart, during the first half of 2018. This provided a crucial base of operations during the early stages.

## Securing Foundational Funding

Financial viability was secured through two critical funding events in 2018. Early in the year, Confluence Urban Systems closed an initial seed funding round totaling $750,000. This capital was primarily sourced from Chicago-based angel investors, including members affiliated with the Hyde Park Angels network, leveraging connections from Dr. Thorne's academic background and the broader Chicago civic tech community.

A second, significant funding milestone was achieved in mid-2018 with the award of a $1.2 million 'Smart City Challenge' grant from the Illinois Department of Commerce and Economic Opportunity (DCEO). The application process for this competitive grant began in late 2017, culminating in the official award notification and funding allocation in May 2018. This grant was specifically designated for establishing the initial pilot sensor networks and the foundational data platform in Chicago. The successful grant proposal explicitly outlined key deliverables for the 2018-2019 period, including the deployment and testing of at least 50 LoRaWAN-enabled environmental sensors intended for an early waste management pilot project.

## Building the Initial Team

By the close of 2018, the Confluence Urban Systems team had grown beyond its co-founders. The initial core team comprised Dr. Marcus Thorne and Anya Sharma, along with three crucial early hires: a data scientist, a software engineer, and a community outreach specialist, establishing the foundational expertise needed for the company's technical development and community engagement efforts.

This period laid the essential groundwork—conceptual, legal, financial, and human—setting the stage for Confluence Urban Systems' subsequent pilot projects and growth.
