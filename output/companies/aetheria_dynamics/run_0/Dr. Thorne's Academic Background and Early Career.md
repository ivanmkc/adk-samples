# Dr. Aris Thorne: Academic Foundations and Early Career at NASA JPL

Dr. Aris Thorne's later success as the founder of Aetheria Dynamics was built upon a strong foundation in aerospace engineering, artificial intelligence, and robotics, honed during his doctoral studies and early professional career at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL).

## Doctoral Studies at Stanford University

Around 2008, Dr. Thorne completed his Ph.D. in Aerospace Engineering at Stanford University, specializing in Artificial Intelligence and Robotics. His dissertation research centered on decentralized control algorithms designed for multi-agent robotic systems. This work proved highly influential, directly shaping his subsequent focus on swarm intelligence. During his doctoral program, Dr. Thorne served as a research assistant within the prestigious Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (SAIL). In this capacity, he contributed to projects focused on the coordination of autonomous aerial vehicles, gaining early experience in the challenges of managing multiple autonomous agents.

## Tenure at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory

Following his Ph.D., Dr. Thorne joined NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) in Pasadena, California, working as an aerospace engineer from approximately mid-2008 until early 2014. Assigned to the Autonomous Systems Division, he made significant contributions to the development and testing of the 'AutoNav' software. This system is responsible for the autonomous driving capabilities of the Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) rover, Curiosity.

His specific technical contributions at JPL involved enhancing algorithms crucial for the rover's safe navigation across the Martian landscape. This included improvements to visual odometry, which helps the rover track its position, and real-time hazard detection using stereo vision data, enabling Curiosity to identify and avoid obstacles during long traverses. In recognition of the team's success in implementing and operating the AutoNav software on Mars, Dr. Thorne shared in receiving a NASA Group Achievement Award in 2013. Throughout his tenure at JPL, he also co-authored several technical reports and conference papers detailing advancements in planetary rover autonomy and sensor fusion techniques.

## Independent Research and Transition

Towards the end of his time at JPL, approximately between 2012 and 2014, Dr. Thorne began conducting independent research into the flight dynamics of starling murmurations. This bio-inspired research laid the groundwork for the core concepts that would later define the Helios AI system. He presented some preliminary concepts for swarm control algorithms derived from this research at the AIAA Guidance, Navigation, and Control Conference in 2013.

Dr. Thorne's decision to leave JPL in early 2014 was driven by a growing conviction. He believed that unlocking the full potential of large-scale autonomous aerial logistics required a fundamentally new approach based on decentralized swarm AI, inspired by his murmurations research. This conviction led him to found Aetheria Dynamics later that same year, marking the end of his early career phase and the beginning of his entrepreneurial journey into autonomous systems.
