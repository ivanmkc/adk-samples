# Early Milestones: Incorporation, Funding, and Prototyping

MindWeave EduSolutions marked its formal beginning in the second quarter of 2019 when it was officially incorporated as a Delaware C-Corporation. Concurrently, the company registered to operate within Massachusetts, establishing its presence in the Boston area, a hub for both technology and education.

Following incorporation, a crucial step was securing the necessary capital to begin operations and development. In late 2019, MindWeave successfully closed its initial seed funding round, raising approximately $750,000. This capital injection was pivotal for the nascent company.

The primary allocation of this seed funding was directed towards building the foundational team and the initial product. Funds were used to hire the first three software engineers, a recruitment effort led by co-founder Ben Carter. By early 2020, engineers with expertise in cloud computing and machine learning were brought on board. This team was tasked with developing the Minimum Viable Product (MVP) of the company's flagship offering, the Synapse Platform.

The first functional prototype tested was this MVP, which strategically focused on validating the core adaptive learning algorithms. Initially, the emphasis was placed on Algebra readiness, addressing a key challenge area identified in K-12 education.

To test the MVP in a real-world setting, MindWeave partnered with two Massachusetts charter schools. The selection of charter schools was a strategic decision, leveraging their characteristic flexibility in adopting new educational technologies compared to larger public districts at that early stage. The pilot program for the Synapse Platform MVP ran throughout the Spring 2020 semester.

Dr. Ananya Sharma, co-founder and former educator, personally oversaw the pedagogical aspects of the MVP pilot. She worked closely with teachers at the partner schools to integrate the Synapse modules effectively into their existing math curriculum. The key objectives for this initial pilot were clearly defined:
*   Gather user feedback on the platform's interface usability.
*   Measure student engagement levels with the adaptive math modules.
*   Collect preliminary data on learning gains attributable to the platform.

The feedback gathered during this crucial pilot phase proved invaluable. It directly influenced the first major iteration of the Synapse Platform's user interface and led to significant refinements in its content recommendation engine, ensuring the platform evolved based on direct user experience and educational impact data.
