# Blue Ridge Outfitters: A Commitment to Environmental Stewardship and Conservation

Blue Ridge Outfitters has woven environmental responsibility into its core identity since its early days. Building on a foundation established over decades, the company actively pursues initiatives aimed at minimizing its operational footprint, supporting conservation organizations, promoting sustainable products, and engaging its community in environmental stewardship.

## Financial Support and Strategic Partnerships

A cornerstone of Blue Ridge Outfitters' environmental commitment is its long-standing practice of dedicating financial resources to conservation efforts.

*   **1% for the Planet:** Fulfilling a pledge initiated in 1990 by former CEO Eleanor Stone-Davis, the company consistently donates 1% of its annual profits to environmental conservation non-profits.
*   **Southern Appalachian Highlands Conservancy (SAHC):** This foundational partnership, established back in 1985, involves direct financial support for critical land acquisition projects, particularly in the Roan Highlands. Blue Ridge Outfitters also provides ongoing sponsorship for SAHC's volunteer trail maintenance programs near its Asheville headquarters.
*   **The Conservation Alliance:** As an active member since the early 2000s, Blue Ridge Outfitters contributes annual dues that pool resources with other member companies. These funds support grassroots conservation projects across North America, specifically focused on protecting wild places vital for outdoor recreation.
*   **Access Fund:** Recognizing the importance of climbing access, the company provides targeted support to the Access Fund. These contributions aid in preserving climbing access and funding stewardship projects in key climbing areas relevant to its customer base, such as the Red River Gorge (Kentucky) and Rifle Mountain Park (Colorado).

## Reducing Operational Impact

Blue Ridge Outfitters actively works to lessen the environmental impact of its facilities and operations.

*   **Waste Diversion:** A comprehensive waste diversion program is implemented across the Asheville distribution center and all flagship stores (Denver, Bend, Jackson, Asheville HQ). The program targets a 25% reduction in landfill waste by 2025 (compared to a 2020 baseline) through robust recycling of cardboard, paper, and plastics, as well as diverting hard-to-recycle items like plastic film wrap via specialized partnerships.
*   **Energy Efficiency:** By the end of 2021, all four flagship stores and the Asheville distribution center completed a full retrofit to energy-efficient LED lighting. This initiative resulted in an estimated 15% reduction in annual electricity consumption specifically for lighting systems.
*   **Renewable Energy:** Furthering its commitment, a 100 kW solar panel array was installed on the roof of the Asheville distribution center in 2022. This system is projected to offset approximately 20% of the facility's annual electricity usage, directly reducing the carbon footprint associated with e-commerce fulfillment operations.

## Sustainable Product Development: Pathfinder Gear

Sustainability is increasingly integrated into the company's private label, Pathfinder Gear.

*   **Bluesign Approved Fabrics:** A significant goal is to have 50% of the Pathfinder Gear apparel line utilize Bluesign approved fabrics by 2026. This effort prioritizes high-volume product categories, including popular items like the 'Blue Ridge' line of fleece jackets, rainwear shells, and core synthetic insulation pieces, ensuring responsible textile production.

## Employee Engagement and Customer Education

Blue Ridge Outfitters fosters environmental stewardship through both internal programs and customer outreach.

*   **'Blue Ridge Gives Back':** The company encourages direct employee involvement through its volunteer program, providing 16 hours of paid time off annually. Employees frequently use this time to participate in trail building days organized by partners like the Southern Appalachian Highlands Conservancy near Asheville, and crag cleanup events coordinated with the Access Fund near the Denver and Bend stores.
*   **Leave No Trace (LNT) Advocacy:** Promoting responsible outdoor ethics is key. Blue Ridge Outfitters offers dedicated free Leave No Trace workshops to customers at flagship stores like Denver and Bend. Furthermore, a mandatory LNT training module is included in the comprehensive 'Basecamp Training' program required for all new retail employees, ensuring staff can effectively educate customers.

Through these multifaceted initiatives – from financial contributions and strategic partnerships to operational improvements, sustainable product goals, and community engagement – Blue Ridge Outfitters demonstrates an ongoing and evolving commitment to protecting the natural environments that are central to its business and passion.
