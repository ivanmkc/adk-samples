# Pathfinder Gear: Advancing Sustainability Through Materials and Circularity

Blue Ridge Outfitters is deepening its commitment to environmental stewardship by setting ambitious future goals for its private label line, Pathfinder Gear. Spearheaded by Marcus Davis and his team, these initiatives focus on significantly increasing the use of sustainable materials and embracing circular economy principles to reduce environmental impact.

## Ambitious Sustainable Material Targets

Pathfinder Gear has established clear targets to transform its material footprint, aiming for **75% of its apparel line to incorporate recycled or renewable materials by 2028**. This represents a significant leap from the estimated baseline of approximately 35% sustainable materials across the apparel line in early 2024.

Key milestones and focus areas include:

*   **Bluesign System Partnership:** Achieving **50% Bluesign approved fabrics by 2026** is considered a crucial stepping stone. This focus ensures responsible chemical management throughout the supply chain, contributing to safer products and manufacturing processes.
*   **Diverse Sustainable Fibers:** The 2028 goal includes specific targets for incorporating a wider range of sustainable options, such as:
    *   Recycled nylon (expanding beyond fishing nets).
    *   Certified responsible wool.
    *   Bio-based polyester alternatives.
*   **Beyond Apparel:** The commitment extends to high-volume product lines outside of clothing. Notably, the popular 'Summit Seeker' backpack series will begin using recycled PET fabrics for the main body starting with the 2026 product cycle.
*   **Sustainable Insulation:** Significant changes are planned for insulation:
    *   Transitioning at least **50% of down insulation** used in sleeping bags and jackets to certified Responsible Down Standard (RDS) recycled down by the end of 2027.
    *   Experimenting with innovative **bio-based synthetic insulation** derived from plant sugars as a potential replacement for traditional petroleum-based fills, with initial trials targeted for select sleeping bags in the 2026 product line.

## Embracing Circularity: The 'Pathfinder Renewed' Program

Recognizing the importance of extending product lifespan, Blue Ridge Outfitters is developing the **'Pathfinder Renewed' gear take-back and resale program**.

*   **Pilot Launch:** A pilot program is slated for **late 2025**, initially focusing on accepting used Pathfinder Gear apparel items like fleece jackets and rain shells. This pilot will launch at the Asheville, NC, and Denver, CO, flagship locations.
*   **Resale Platform:** Collected gear will be professionally cleaned and graded based on condition (e.g., 'Excellent', 'Good', 'Functional'). These renewed items will then be resold through a dedicated section on the blueridgeoutfitters.com website.
*   **Logistics and Partnerships:** To manage the complexities of collection, cleaning, potential repair, and resale, Blue Ridge Outfitters is actively evaluating partnerships with established textile recycling and resale companies, such as Renewal Workshop or Trove.

## Design for Repairability: Supporting Longevity

Underpinning the circularity goals is a dedicated focus on **'Design for Repairability'** within the Pathfinder Gear development process. By ensuring that components like zippers and buckles on backpacks and apparel are more easily replaceable, the design team aims to extend the usable life of products. This directly supports the 'Pathfinder Renewed' program by increasing the pool of items suitable for refurbishment and resale, keeping valuable materials in use for longer.

Through this integrated approach—setting aggressive sustainable material targets, launching the 'Pathfinder Renewed' circularity program, and embedding design for repairability—Blue Ridge Outfitters and its Pathfinder Gear line are taking concrete steps towards a more sustainable future in the outdoor industry.
