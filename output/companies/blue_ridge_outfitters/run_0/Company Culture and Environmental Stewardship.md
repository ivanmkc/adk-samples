# Cultivating Responsibility: Environmental Stewardship and Company Culture at Blue Ridge Outfitters

Blue Ridge Outfitters (BRO) has built its identity not just on selling quality outdoor gear, but also on a deeply ingrained commitment to environmental stewardship and fostering a supportive, knowledgeable company culture. These values, woven into the fabric of the family-owned business since its inception, guide its operations, product development, and community interactions.

## A Legacy of Environmental Commitment

The company's dedication to conservation was formally solidified in 1990 under the leadership of then-CEO Eleanor Stone-Davis. At that time, Blue Ridge Outfitters pledged to donate 1% of its annual profits to non-profit organizations focused on conservation. This commitment builds upon earlier efforts, such as the first major conservation partnership established in 1985 with the Southern Appalachian Highlands Conservancy (SAHC).

Today, this support continues robustly. Beyond the long-standing relationship with SAHC, BRO actively contributes financially and participates in events supporting national groups like The Conservation Alliance and the Access Fund, organizations dedicated to protecting wild places and climbing access.

This environmental ethos extends directly into the company's operations. All flagship retail stores – located in Denver, CO; Bend, OR; Jackson, WY; and the Asheville, NC headquarters – have undergone retrofits to install energy-efficient LED lighting. Furthermore, comprehensive waste diversion programs are in place at these locations, with a specific target to reduce landfill waste by 25% by the year 2025, using 2020 as a baseline.

Sustainability is also a key driver in product development. The 'Pathfinder Gear' private label team, led by Marcus Davis, is actively pursuing a Bluesign system partnership for its textile supply chain. The ambitious goal is for 50% of the Pathfinder Gear apparel line to utilize Bluesign approved fabrics by 2026, complementing existing efforts to use recycled materials and PFC-free DWR finishes. Looking ahead, the planned network of Blue Ridge Outfitters eco-lodges near national parks is being designed with stringent environmental criteria, aiming for LEED certification, incorporation of renewable energy sources like solar power, and advanced water conservation systems.

## Fostering Expertise and Engagement Within

Blue Ridge Outfitters places significant emphasis on developing and retaining knowledgeable staff who are passionate about the outdoors. New retail employees undergo a mandatory 40-hour 'Basecamp Training' program. This comprehensive onboarding covers core product knowledge, specialized skills like advanced boot fitting and pack fitting techniques, and crucially, the principles of Leave No Trace ethics. This foundational training is supplemented by seasonal clinics hosted by vendor partners.

To encourage authentic expertise and personal experience with the gear they sell, full-time employees receive an annual gear stipend. They also benefit from substantial product discounts, including up to 60% off the company's own Pathfinder Gear line, and access to pro-deal programs from respected partner brands such as Patagonia and Black Diamond.

The company culture actively encourages giving back. Through the 'Blue Ridge Gives Back' program, employees are provided with 16 hours of paid time off annually to volunteer for approved environmental or community partners. This often involves participating in local trail maintenance days or river cleanup events.

A strong tradition of promoting from within reinforces employee loyalty and career development. Notably, the current CEO, Amelia Davis, began her career within the company's marketing department. Similarly, several current store managers started their journeys as part-time retail associates, demonstrating clear pathways for growth within Blue Ridge Outfitters.

## Connecting with the Community

Beyond internal culture and environmental action, BRO actively engages with its local communities. Each flagship store serves as a hub, hosting an average of five free community workshops or events per month. Popular topics cater to a range of outdoor interests, including 'Introduction to Backcountry Navigation using GPS,' 'Campfire Cooking Techniques,' and, in relevant mountain locations like Denver and Jackson, 'Avalanche Awareness Basics.' These events not only provide valuable skills but also strengthen the connection between the store and local outdoor enthusiasts.

In essence, Blue Ridge Outfitters strives to operate as more than just a retailer. Its formalized environmental commitments, sustainable operational practices, investment in employee expertise and well-being, and active community engagement collectively define a company culture deeply rooted in responsibility towards both the planet and its people.
