# Connecting with Communities: Engagement and Volunteerism at Blue Ridge Outfitters

Blue Ridge Outfitters extends its commitment beyond retail, actively fostering connections within the communities it serves and empowering its employees to contribute through dedicated volunteer efforts. This engagement manifests through regular store events and a robust employee volunteer program.

## Community Hubs: Workshops and Events

Flagship Blue Ridge Outfitters locations function as more than just stores; they are community hubs offering free educational workshops and engaging events. These sessions aim to enhance outdoor skills and appreciation among customers and the local community.

*   **Skills Workshops:** Standard free workshops, such as 'Introduction to Backcountry Navigation' or 'Campfire Cooking Techniques', are regularly hosted. These sessions typically see an average attendance of 15 to 25 participants, providing accessible learning opportunities.
*   **Local Partnerships:** Stores often collaborate with local organizations to deliver specialized content. For instance, the Denver flagship store partners with the Colorado Fourteeners Initiative for trail stewardship workshops, while the Bend store works alongside the Deschutes Trails Coalition on similar initiatives.
*   **Beyond Workshops:** Community engagement extends to other events. The Denver store, utilizing its 50-seat presentation space, hosts monthly adventure film screenings, often featuring selections from renowned events like the Banff Mountain Film Festival World Tour.
*   **Promotion:** Information about upcoming workshops and events is actively disseminated through various channels to ensure community awareness. This includes dedicated email newsletters segmented by store location, updates on store-specific social media accounts (like @BROBend), and prominent event calendars displayed within each store.

## Giving Back: The Employee Volunteer Program

Blue Ridge Outfitters encourages its staff to be active community members through the 'Blue Ridge Gives Back' employee volunteer program.

*   **Program Launch and Structure:** Officially launched company-wide in 2010 under the leadership of then-CEO Eleanor Stone-Davis, the program provides 16 hours of annual paid time off specifically for volunteering.
*   **Eligibility:** This benefit is available to all full-time employees who have successfully completed their initial 90-day probationary period.
*   **Tracking and Participation:** Employees utilize a dedicated internal company portal to log their volunteer hours. This system allows them to specify the partner organization they worked with (such as the Southern Appalachian Highlands Conservancy or the Access Fund) and detail the activity performed.
*   **Local Impact:** The program facilitates direct employee involvement in local conservation and community efforts. Near the Jackson, WY store, employees often use their volunteer hours with groups like the Teton Climbers' Coalition for crag stewardship or assist with events supporting the Grand Teton National Park Foundation. Similarly, the Asheville store frequently partners with RiverLink, organizing employee volunteer groups for French Broad River cleanup events using 'Blue Ridge Gives Back' hours.
*   **Collective Contribution:** The company tracks the overall impact of this initiative. In the fiscal year 2023, Blue Ridge Outfitters reported that its staff collectively contributed over 2,500 volunteer hours through the 'Blue Ridge Gives Back' program, demonstrating a significant commitment to the communities where they live and work.

Through these combined efforts—hosting educational events and facilitating employee volunteerism—Blue Ridge Outfitters actively strengthens its ties to local communities and supports environmental stewardship.
