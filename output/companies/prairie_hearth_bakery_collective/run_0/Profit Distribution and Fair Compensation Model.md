# Prairie Hearth's Model for Profit Distribution and Fair Compensation

Prairie Hearth Bakery Collective operates on principles of economic democracy, deeply embedded in its structure as a worker-owned cooperative. Central to this commitment is a carefully designed system for fair compensation and profit distribution, ensuring that the rewards of collective labor are shared equitably among those who contribute it. This model encompasses both a compressed wage scale and a patronage dividend system tied directly to work hours.

## Fair Compensation: The Compressed Wage Scale

Prairie Hearth maintains a commitment to internal pay equity through a compressed wage scale, mandated by its bylaws. This structure ensures that the compensation of the highest-paid worker-owner never exceeds three times that of the lowest-paid full-time employee.

As of 2024, this commitment translates to a starting wage for entry-level full-time positions set at approximately $22 per hour. The highest possible wage within the collective is capped at roughly $66 per hour, strictly adhering to the 3:1 ratio.

To ensure the wage scale remains fair and relevant, it undergoes a formal review every two years. This review is led by the cooperative's Finance Committee, which considers factors such as changes in the Minneapolis cost of living, industry benchmarks for similar positions, and internal equity considerations. The committee then presents its adjustment recommendations to the General Assembly for approval.

## Profit Sharing: Patronage Dividends Based on Labor

Beyond fair wages, Prairie Hearth shares its financial success directly with its worker-owners through patronage dividends. This system reflects the cooperative principle that those who contribute labor should share in the profits generated.

**Allocation Process:**
At the end of each fiscal year (December 31st), a portion of the net profit is allocated for reinvestment back into the cooperative (typically between 20-30%) to fund future growth, equipment upgrades, or build reserves. The remaining distributable profit is designated for patronage dividends. The specific percentage split between reinvestment and dividends is determined annually by a formal vote at the Q4 General Assembly meeting. This decision follows a recommendation from the Finance Committee, based on the year's financial performance and projected future needs.

**Calculation and Eligibility:**
Patronage dividends are calculated based solely on the labor contribution of each worker-owner. The calculation considers the total number of hours worked by an individual worker-owner during the fiscal year relative to the total hours worked by all worker-owners combined. This method creates a direct link between an individual's work effort and their share of the distributable profit. Importantly, the $1,000 equity investment required for worker-ownership functions as member capital and does not factor into the dividend calculation.

Eligibility for patronage dividends is exclusive to worker-owners. Employees who are still in their candidacy period or choose not to pursue ownership are compensated according to the established wage scale but do not receive these profit distributions.

**Distribution and Exceptional Circumstances:**
The distribution of patronage dividends typically occurs annually, within 90 days following the close of the fiscal year, once the year-end financial statements are finalized and approved by the General Assembly. Worker-owners receive an IRS Form 1099-PATR detailing their dividend amount, as these distributions constitute taxable income derived from their participation in the cooperative.

In years where profits significantly exceed projections (e.g., by more than 15%), the General Assembly has the authority to vote on distributing a larger portion of the net profit as patronage dividends, potentially reducing the reinvestment percentage for that specific year.

## Transparency and Financial Literacy

Prairie Hearth emphasizes transparency and ensures members understand the financial mechanisms of the cooperative. Financial literacy training is provided to new members, covering how to read the cooperative's quarterly financial statements, how profitability impacts potential patronage dividends, and the mechanics of the compressed wage scale. This education empowers worker-owners to participate meaningfully in financial discussions and decisions within the General Assembly.

By combining a fair, compressed wage scale with a profit distribution system based directly on labor contribution, Prairie Hearth Bakery Collective puts its cooperative values into practice, fostering a workplace built on equity, transparency, and shared success.
