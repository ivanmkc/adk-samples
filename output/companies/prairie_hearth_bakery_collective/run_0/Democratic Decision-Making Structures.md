# Democratic Decision-Making at Prairie Hearth Bakery Collective

As a worker-owned cooperative established in 2004, Prairie Hearth Bakery Collective embeds democratic principles into its core operational fabric. The structures for decision-making, largely formalized in the bylaws ratified in 2005, ensure that worker-owners collectively guide the direction and management of the business.

## The General Assembly: Collective Strategic Direction

The highest governing body at Prairie Hearth is the General Assembly (GA), comprising all worker-owners. These meetings are held quarterly and serve as the primary forum for major strategic decisions, such as approving the annual budget or significant expansion plans. To ensure broad participation and legitimacy for these key decisions, a quorum is required, necessitating the physical or virtual presence of at least two-thirds (approximately 67%) of all current worker-owners.

Prairie Hearth employs a formal consensus decision-making process during GA meetings. The goal is to reach full agreement through structured discussion. However, recognizing that complete consensus may not always be achievable, the 2005 bylaws provide a fallback mechanism: if consensus cannot be reached, decisions can still be passed with a 'supermajority' vote of 80% of the worker-owners present at the meeting.

## The Coordinating Committee: Operational Leadership and Implementation

While the GA sets the strategic direction, day-to-day operational coordination and the implementation of GA decisions are delegated to an elected Coordinating Committee (CC). This committee consists of 5 worker-owners who serve staggered terms. Elections for the CC are held annually during the Q4 General Assembly meeting. Worker-owners nominate candidates, and voting occurs via secret ballot, with the candidates receiving the highest number of votes being elected (typically two or three positions are open each year due to the staggered terms).

The Coordinating Committee meets weekly, usually on Tuesday mornings, to address ongoing operational matters, review performance metrics from the previous week, and prepare reports or proposals for upcoming General Assembly meetings. Specific responsibilities delegated to the CC include approving non-budgeted expenditures under $5,000, finalizing the weekly production schedules, and managing the day-to-day relationships with key farm suppliers, such as Meadowlark Organics.

## Standing Committees: Focused Expertise

Beyond the Coordinating Committee, Prairie Hearth maintains standing committees established by the bylaws to handle specific areas. Key among these are:
*   **Finance Committee:** Responsible for preparing the annual budget proposal for the GA and ongoing financial monitoring throughout the year.
*   **Membership Committee:** Oversees the process for employees becoming worker-owners (the candidacy process) and focuses on member engagement initiatives.

## Bylaws, Transparency, and Conflict Resolution

The foundational rules governing Prairie Hearth – the bylaws originally ratified in 2005 – can be amended through a democratic process. Amendments require a formal proposal submitted by at least three worker-owners, followed by a minimum 30-day discussion period involving all members. Final approval must be achieved by consensus (or the 80% supermajority fallback) at a General Assembly meeting.

Transparency is a key principle. Minutes from all General Assembly and Coordinating Committee meetings are formally recorded, approved at the subsequent meeting, and archived electronically. These records are accessible to all worker-owners via an internal shared drive.

Workplace conflicts are addressed through a peer-based mediation process, also established in 2005. This system utilizes a rotating pool of trained worker-owner mediators who facilitate conflict resolution sessions according to specific steps outlined in the cooperative's internal conflict resolution policy.

## Training and Reinforcement

To ensure all members can participate effectively, the governance training module for new worker-owners includes specific instruction on facilitating meetings using the cooperative's consensus model. It also covers understanding the financial reports presented by the Finance Committee at General Assemblies, empowering members with the knowledge needed for informed decision-making.

These interconnected structures – from the broad strategic direction set by the General Assembly to the operational focus of the Coordinating Committee and the specialized work of standing committees, all underpinned by transparent processes and participatory mechanisms – embody Prairie Hearth's commitment to democratic worker control.
