# Cultivating Connections: Prairie Hearth's Local Sourcing and Farm Partnerships

A cornerstone of Prairie Hearth Bakery Collective's identity and mission is its deep commitment to supporting local agriculture through direct relationships with regional farms. This dedication goes beyond simply using local ingredients; it involves building robust partnerships that strengthen the regional food system and ensure the highest quality inputs for their artisanal breads and pastries.

## Scope and Commitment

Prairie Hearth actively maintains direct purchasing relationships with over 15 different farms and orchards, primarily situated within a 150-mile radius of Minneapolis. This commitment is exemplified by their flour sourcing: as of 2024, approximately 80% of the bakery's total flour volume comes directly from Minnesota and Wisconsin farms. This significantly surpasses the collective's original goal, set back in 2004, of sourcing 50% of their flour locally.

## Key Supplier Relationships

The bakery has established strong ties with specific producers for essential ingredients:

*   **Grains:** Key grain suppliers include Meadowlark Organics in Wisconsin, providing heritage Red Fife wheat, and Hazzard Free Farm in Minnesota, supplying organic rye and spelt. These relationships are crucial for accessing the unique heirloom grains featured in Prairie Hearth's signature loaves.
*   **Dairy:** All dairy products – milk, cream, and butter – used in pastries and other baked goods are sourced exclusively from Cedar Summit Farm, a well-regarded grass-fed dairy located near New Prague, Minnesota.
*   **Honey:** The honey featured in products like the Honey Whole Wheat loaf comes from Bare Honey, a Minneapolis-based apiary known for its distinct single-origin honey varieties.

## Building Strong Partnerships

Prairie Hearth fosters its farm connections through proactive engagement. The bakery often utilizes pre-season contracts, guaranteeing the purchase of specific quantities of produce like heirloom tomatoes or particular apple varieties such as Honeycrisp. This practice provides financial stability for farmers and ensures the bakery secures desired seasonal ingredients. Furthermore, Prairie Hearth bakers conduct annual visits to key partner farms, like their primary berry supplier in western Wisconsin, to deepen relationships and gain firsthand understanding of agricultural practices.

## Seasonal Sourcing and Sustainable Practices

Beyond staple ingredients, the collective sources seasonal vegetables, such as zucchini and winter squash for specialty breads and savory items, directly from growers participating in the Mill City Farmers Market in Minneapolis. Crucially, Prairie Hearth prioritizes partnerships with farms employing certified organic or sustainable agricultural methods. This aligns directly with the collective's broader mission of environmental stewardship and bolstering the resilience of the regional food economy.

## Innovation and Regional Adaptation

Prairie Hearth also engages in forward-looking collaborations. The bakery occasionally works with the University of Minnesota's agricultural research programs, trialing small batches of newly developed or rediscovered heirloom grain varieties, like the perennial grain Kernza®, that are well-suited to the regional climate. This demonstrates a commitment not only to tradition but also to the future of sustainable agriculture in the Midwest.
