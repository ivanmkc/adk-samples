# Prairie Hearth Explores Cooperative Expansion: Conversion Model and New Markets

Building on its 20-year legacy as a worker-owned cooperative rooted in Minneapolis, Prairie Hearth Bakery Collective is actively exploring strategic expansion into new Midwestern cities. Rather than solely focusing on opening new company-owned branches, the collective is undertaking a comprehensive feasibility study centered on a unique "cooperative conversion" model. This approach aims to partner with existing independent bakeries, assisting them in transitioning to a worker-cooperative structure aligned with Prairie Hearth's principles.

## The Expansion Subcommittee and Feasibility Study

Overseeing this initiative is a dedicated Expansion Subcommittee, formally established by the Prairie Hearth General Assembly in late 2023. Co-founder David Rodriguez, known for his background in community organizing and food justice, chairs this subcommittee. The group is tasked with managing the 2024-2025 feasibility study, evaluating the viability of the conversion model, and identifying promising locations and partners.

## The Cooperative Conversion Model

The core idea involves identifying established bakeries that resonate with Prairie Hearth's values and assisting their transition into independent worker cooperatives operating under the Prairie Hearth brand. Key criteria outlined by the Expansion Subcommittee for selecting potential conversion partners include:

*   A minimum of three years of continuous operation.
*   A demonstrated commitment to sourcing at least 30% of ingredients locally or regionally.
*   Formal written interest from the current ownership in exploring a worker-cooperative transition.

## Market Analysis: Madison and Duluth

Preliminary market analysis has focused on two potential cities: Madison, Wisconsin, and Duluth, Minnesota.

### Madison, Wisconsin

Analysis favors Madison due to several factors. The city boasts a strong existing customer base familiar with cooperative models, evidenced by the presence of three established food cooperatives, including the prominent Willy Street Co-op. Furthermore, Madison's strategic location, less than 150 miles from key Prairie Hearth grain supplier Meadowlark Organics, presents logistical advantages for maintaining sourcing standards.

### Duluth, Minnesota

The assessment of Duluth highlights different opportunities. Its distinct regional identity and potential for partnerships with North Shore food producers are seen as strengths. A competitive analysis completed in Q1 2024 also indicated a less saturated artisanal bakery market compared to the Twin Cities, potentially offering a clearer niche for Prairie Hearth's model.

Market research conducted in early 2024 for both cities included a detailed analysis of the top three competing bakeries in each location. This research specifically evaluated competitors' product offerings, price points, sourcing transparency, and whether they utilized long fermentation techniques or operated under cooperative principles.

## Proposed Structure and Support

The Expansion Subcommittee is exploring the optimal framework for these potential conversions.

### Legal Structure

Preliminary discussions favor a licensing agreement model. Under this structure, the converted bakery would operate as an independent cooperative entity. It would adhere to Prairie Hearth's core principles and branding standards while maintaining its autonomy as a worker-owned business. The subcommittee aims to recommend a preferred legal structure by the first quarter of 2025.

### Conversion Toolkit

To facilitate successful transitions, Prairie Hearth envisions offering a "conversion toolkit" to new worker-owners. This support package would include:

*   Access to Prairie Hearth's proprietary training modules on cooperative finance and governance, developed in collaboration with Cooperative Development Services (CDS).
*   Standardized recipes for signature items, such as the renowned 'North Star Sourdough'.
*   Facilitated access to Prairie Hearth's established relationships and negotiated contracts with key suppliers like Hazzard Free Farm and Cedar Summit Farm.

### Financial and Technical Assistance

Prairie Hearth's leadership has also initiated exploratory talks with the Northcountry Cooperative Development Fund (NCDF). These discussions focus on potential financial products or technical assistance grants that could help meet the capital needs of bakeries undergoing the conversion process.

## Next Steps and Strategic Vision

The feasibility study is ongoing. The Expansion Subcommittee has budgeted for and plans to conduct detailed, multi-day site visits to at least one promising potential conversion candidate in both Madison and Duluth before the end of the third quarter of 2025.

Pending the results of the feasibility study, Prairie Hearth's current strategic roadmap prioritizes launching a pilot cooperative conversion project as the primary mode of expansion into a new city. This pilot, potentially launching by early 2026, would precede any consideration of establishing a new Prairie Hearth-owned-and-operated branch. This phased approach underscores the collective's commitment to fostering cooperative principles and strengthening regional food systems through partnership rather than solely through direct ownership.
