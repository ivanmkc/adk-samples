# Prairie Hearth's Worker Cooperative Model and Governance

Prairie Hearth Bakery Collective operates on a worker-owned cooperative model, placing democratic control and shared ownership at the heart of its structure. This approach shapes everything from strategic decision-making to daily operations and compensation.

## Becoming a Worker-Owner

The path to worker-ownership at Prairie Hearth is designed to ensure commitment and understanding of the cooperative's values.
*   **Eligibility:** Employees typically become eligible for consideration after a one-year candidacy period. During this time, they must demonstrate a commitment to cooperative principles.
*   **Approval:** Existing worker-owners vote on admitting new members, requiring a majority approval.
*   **Equity Investment:** Upon approval, new worker-owners make a $1,000 equity investment. This contribution, often payable through payroll deductions over their first two years of ownership, symbolizes their shared stake and commitment to the collective enterprise.

## Democratic Governance Structure

Decision-making power rests fundamentally with the worker-owners.
*   **General Assembly:** The highest governing body is the General Assembly, comprising all worker-owners. They meet quarterly to deliberate and vote on major strategic decisions. Crucial matters, such as approving the annual budget or deciding on significant expansion plans like the proposed line of packaged baking mixes, require a consensus vote among all worker-owners present.
*   **Coordinating Committee:** To manage day-to-day operations and implement the decisions made by the General Assembly, a Coordinating Committee is elected. This committee consists of 5 worker-owners who serve staggered two-year terms, ensuring continuity and effective operational coordination.

## Shared Benefits and Responsibilities

Membership in the cooperative entails both shared rewards and active duties.
*   **Profit Distribution:** Prairie Hearth employs a specific model for distributing year-end profits. Typically, 20-30% of profits are allocated for reinvestment back into the business and building reserves. The remaining portion is distributed to worker-owners as patronage dividends, calculated proportionally based on the hours each member worked during the year.
*   **Equitable Compensation:** Reinforcing its commitment to equitable labor practices, the cooperative maintains a compressed wage scale. This policy ensures that the compensation of the highest-paid worker-owner does not exceed three times that of the lowest-paid full-time employee.
*   **Member Participation and Responsibilities:** Worker-owners have clearly defined responsibilities outlined in the cooperative's bylaws. These include mandatory attendance at General Assembly meetings, active participation in committee work, and a commitment to upholding the cooperative's mission and values. As of its 20th anniversary in 2024, this model has fostered significant engagement, with approximately 75% of Prairie Hearth's full-time staff being worker-owners.

## Maintaining the Cooperative Culture

Prairie Hearth actively cultivates its cooperative identity through education and established processes.
*   **Training and Education:** All new hires receive comprehensive training within their first three months. This covers the seven International Cooperative Alliance principles, Prairie Hearth's specific bylaws, and financial literacy relevant to understanding the cooperative business model.
*   **Conflict Resolution:** Workplace conflicts among members are primarily addressed through a peer-based mediation process. This system, detailed in internal bylaws first ratified in 2005, provides a structured and cooperative approach to resolving disagreements.

Through these structures and practices, Prairie Hearth embodies its founding commitment to building a democratic workplace, ensuring that those who contribute their labor also share in the governance and success of the enterprise.
