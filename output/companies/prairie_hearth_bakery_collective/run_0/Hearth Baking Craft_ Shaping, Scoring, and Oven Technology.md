# The Craft of Hearth Baking: Shaping, Scoring, and Oven Technology at Prairie Hearth

The creation of exceptional hearth bread, like the signature loaves found at Prairie Hearth Bakery Collective, relies on a mastery of techniques that extend beyond ingredient selection and fermentation. The crucial stages of shaping, scoring, and the precise use of specialized ovens are fundamental to achieving the desired texture, appearance, and volume of artisanal bread.

## Meticulous Shaping for Optimal Structure

At Prairie Hearth, bakers employ a deliberate two-stage shaping process for their hearth loaves. Following the bulk fermentation period, the dough is first gently pre-shaped into a round. This initial form rests on the bench for 20-30 minutes, allowing the gluten to relax before the final shaping. This second stage is critical for building optimal surface tension, where the dough is skillfully formed into either a tight round boule or an elongated bâtard.

Handling high-hydration doughs, such as the popular 75% hydration 'North Star Sourdough', requires specific techniques. Prairie Hearth bakers minimize the use of dusting flour and rely on plastic or metal bench scrapers to effectively manage the sticky dough, ensuring it can be shaped correctly without incorporating excess flour, which could compromise the crumb structure.

## The Art and Science of Scoring

Immediately before the loaves are loaded into the hot ovens, bakers perform the essential step of scoring. Using a specialized tool called a lame, which holds a sharp, often curved razor blade, precise cuts are made onto the loaf's surface. This strategic scoring serves a vital purpose: it creates controlled weak points, guiding the dough's rapid expansion, known as "oven spring," during the initial phase of baking. Without scoring, the crust could tear or burst unpredictably as the loaf expands in the intense heat.

Different loaves receive distinct patterns. The signature 'North Star Sourdough' boule, for instance, is commonly scored with a four-quadrant 'cross' or a square pattern. These cuts, typically 1/4 to 1/2 inch deep, not only control the expansion but also help create the characteristic raised edge or 'ear' sought after in artisanal loaves.

## European Deck Ovens and the Baking Environment

Prairie Hearth utilizes European-style deck ovens, the workhorses of traditional hearth baking. These ovens feature stone baking surfaces, or hearths, which are critical for achieving a well-baked bottom crust and contributing significantly to the upward lift or 'oven spring' through intense radiant heat transfer.

The baking process begins with the ovens preheated to a high temperature, typically between 475-500°F (245-260°C). As the loaves are loaded onto the hot stone, steam injection is employed for the first 15-20 minutes of the bake cycle. This steam delays the formation of the crust, allowing the loaf to expand to its maximum volume. It also contributes to the development of the thin, crackling, often blistered crust characteristic of Prairie Hearth's breads.

Following the initial steam phase, the oven vents are opened to release the moisture, and the temperature is often lowered to around 425-450°F (220-230°C). This allows the crust to achieve a deep golden-brown color and ensures the interior crumb structure fully sets. A standard 1kg Prairie Hearth sourdough loaf typically bakes for 35-45 minutes. Doneness is confirmed when the loaf reaches a target internal temperature between 205-210°F (96-99°C).

Through the careful application of these shaping, scoring, and baking techniques within their specialized deck ovens, the bakers at Prairie Hearth transform carefully fermented dough into loaves with exceptional structure, crust, and crumb – the hallmarks of true hearth baking craft.
