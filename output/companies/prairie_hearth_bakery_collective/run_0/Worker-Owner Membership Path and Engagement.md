## Becoming and Engaging as a Worker-Owner at Prairie Hearth

Prairie Hearth Bakery Collective operates on a foundation of shared ownership and democratic participation. The path to becoming a worker-owner and the ongoing engagement expected are clearly defined processes designed to integrate new members fully into the cooperative's structure and culture.

### The Path to Worker-Ownership: The Candidacy Period

Prospective worker-owners typically embark on a one-year candidacy period. This crucial time serves as an introduction to the cooperative model and Prairie Hearth's specific operations. During this year, each candidate is paired with an experienced worker-owner mentor. This mentor provides guidance on cooperative principles, helps the candidate navigate the participatory culture, and supports their integration into the collective.

To ensure candidates are progressing in their understanding and involvement, the Membership Committee conducts formal check-ins at the 6-month and 11-month marks. These meetings assess the candidate's grasp of cooperative values and their level of participation within the bakery. Based on these assessments, the Membership Committee makes a recommendation regarding the candidate's readiness for the ownership vote.

### The Ownership Vote

The decision to admit a new worker-owner is made collectively by the existing members. The vote takes place during a General Assembly meeting. Following a presentation by the Membership Committee summarizing the candidate's journey and qualifications, and a question-and-answer session with the candidate, a vote is held via secret ballot. Approval requires a simple majority (over 50%) of the voting worker-owners present at the meeting.

### Responsibilities and Engagement of Worker-Owners

Achieving worker-ownership comes with both rights and responsibilities, emphasizing active participation:

*   **Meeting Attendance:** As stipulated in the bylaws revised in 2018, worker-owners are expected to attend at least 75% of the quarterly General Assembly meetings each year. Attendance can be either in person or virtual, ensuring members can maintain active participation status.
*   **Committee Work:** Within their first six months of ownership, new worker-owners are required to join at least one standing committee, such as Finance, Membership, Marketing, or Farm Relations. This involves contributing an average of 2-4 hours per month to committee responsibilities, playing a direct role in specific operational areas.
*   **Financial Transparency:** All worker-owners have the right to access detailed quarterly financial statements and the annual operating budget. These documents are typically reviewed and discussed during General Assembly meetings, often facilitated by the Finance Committee, ensuring transparency in the collective's financial health.

### Addressing Unsuccessful Candidacy

If a candidate does not achieve the required majority vote after their initial one-year candidacy, the process doesn't necessarily end. The Membership Committee provides the candidate with specific, constructive feedback. Depending on the circumstances, the committee may propose an extended candidacy period, typically lasting an additional 3 to 6 months, or outline concrete steps the candidate can take to improve and potentially seek a vote again in the future.

### Departure and Equity Return

When a worker-owner decides to leave Prairie Hearth, their financial stake in the cooperative is addressed according to the bylaws. The $1,000 equity investment made upon joining is typically returned to the departing member within 90 days of their official departure date. This return is subject to the deduction of any outstanding financial obligations the member may have towards the cooperative.

The membership path and engagement structure at Prairie Hearth underscore the cooperative's commitment to informed participation, shared responsibility, and democratic governance.
