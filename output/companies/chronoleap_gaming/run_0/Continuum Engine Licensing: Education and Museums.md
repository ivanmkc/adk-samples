# Continuum Engine Licensing: Education and Museums

While ChronoLeap Gaming is renowned for its immersive historical video games, the proprietary Continuum Engine technology underpinning these experiences possesses significant applications beyond entertainment. From the company's inception, the founders envisioned a dual purpose for their platform: delivering unparalleled historical gaming and serving as a powerful tool for education and museum exhibits, aiming to foster deeper historical understanding through direct, embodied experience.

## Empowering Educational Institutions

ChronoLeap actively licenses the Continuum Engine to educational institutions, targeting not only History departments but also Archaeology and Classics programs. The engine's capability to reconstruct ancient sites like Pompeii or Athens, leveraging the vast Chronos Database containing Lidar scans and excavation reports, makes it a valuable tool for research and teaching.

### Licensing Models and Reach

The standard educational licensing model involves a per-seat annual fee, typically starting around $5,000, which covers up to 10 concurrent users per installation. Recognizing the needs of larger institutions, ChronoLeap often negotiates site licenses, enabling broader access across multiple university labs or classrooms. This approach facilitated the adoption of the technology in prominent academic settings, leading to recognition such as the 'Innovation in Education Award' at the 2029 Serious Play Conference.

### Case Study: Stanford University Research

Stanford University's History Department provides a key example of the Continuum Engine's academic application. Postgraduate researchers utilize the engine to run complex simulations based on Chronos Database datasets, particularly focusing on medieval Europe. This allows them to test counterfactual historical scenarios, such as modeling the economic impact of different medieval English succession laws by modifying Logos AI parameters and utilizing Chronos Database economic data.

A crucial component of Stanford's license agreement is access to a specialized API for the Chronos Database. This allows history researchers to directly query and extract anonymized simulation data, enabling quantitative analysis of historical models and furthering academic inquiry.

### Data Privacy and Compliance

Recognizing the sensitivity of research involving student interactions, data privacy agreements are integral to university licenses. ChronoLeap ensures that any student interaction data used for research purposes, as seen at Stanford, is thoroughly anonymized and adheres strictly to institutional review board (IRB) protocols before being accessed via the Chronos API.

## Transforming Museum Experiences

Museums represent another significant sector for Continuum Engine licensing, offering unique ways to engage visitors with history.

### Licensing and Development

Museum licensing typically differs from the educational model. It often involves a substantial one-time project development fee, ranging from $150,000 to $500,000 based on the exhibit's complexity. This is usually coupled with a revenue-sharing agreement, where ChronoLeap receives 10-15% of the ticket revenue specifically attributed to the Continuum Engine-powered exhibit.

### Case Study: The British Museum's 'Walk Like an Egyptian'

A prominent example is the British Museum's 'Walk Like an Egyptian' virtual exhibit. This installation utilizes a customized Continuum Engine build to allow visitors to explore a detailed reconstruction of the Temple of Karnak. To cater to a diverse audience with varying levels of technological familiarity, the exhibit features simplified controls and pre-set pathways alongside free-roam options. Since its public opening in 2028, the exhibit has proven popular, averaging approximately 500 users per day.

### Curator Tools and Ongoing Support

To empower museum staff, ChronoLeap provides specialized development tools, internally known as the 'Curator's Canvas'. This toolkit offers intuitive drag-and-drop interfaces, allowing curators to place scans of their own artifacts directly into the simulation environment. It also includes simplified scripting tools for creating guided virtual tours within the engine.

Furthermore, museum licenses can include ongoing support. The British Museum's agreement, for instance, includes support from ChronoLeap's historical research wing. This ensures that the exhibit remains accurate and up-to-date, incorporating new Egyptological findings as they are integrated into the Chronos Database.

## Support Structures for Institutions

ChronoLeap offers tiered support packages tailored to the needs of institutional licensees. Options range from basic technical support (around $1,000 per year per installation) to premium packages (costing $10,000 or more annually). These premium tiers can include valuable additions like on-site training for museum staff or university faculty, and bespoke assistance in developing specific historical scenarios for educational or exhibit purposes.

## Conclusion: Fostering Historical Understanding

By licensing the Continuum Engine to universities and museums, ChronoLeap extends its core mission beyond gaming. These partnerships leverage the engine's power for historical reconstruction, simulation, and data analysis, providing invaluable tools for research, education, and public engagement. Through tailored licensing models, specialized tools like the 'Curator's Canvas', and dedicated support, ChronoLeap facilitates new ways for students, researchers, and the public to connect with and understand the past, fulfilling the founders' vision of using immersive technology to cultivate historical empathy and knowledge.
