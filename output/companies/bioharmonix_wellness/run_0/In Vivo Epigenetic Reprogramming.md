# In Vivo Epigenetic Reprogramming at BioHarmonix: Project Chronos Rewound

BioHarmonix is actively engaged in pioneering research focused on *in vivo* epigenetic reprogramming, a cutting-edge approach aimed at reversing biological age markers directly within living tissues. This ambitious initiative, internally codenamed "Project Chronos Rewound," reflects the company's core philosophy of treating aging as a solvable challenge and is spearheaded by Dr. Jian Li's research team.

## Core Technology and Delivery System

The foundation of Project Chronos Rewound relies on the controlled, transient expression of the Yamanaka factors (Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, Myc - collectively known as OSKM). To deliver these factors safely and effectively *in vivo*, BioHarmonix utilizes a proprietary, heavily modified Adeno-Associated Virus serotype, designated 'AAV-BHX-EpiReg'. This non-integrating viral vector has been specifically engineered for enhanced tropism towards target cells, initially retinal ganglion cells, and features reduced immunogenicity to minimize host immune responses.

Precision control over the reprogramming process is paramount. This is achieved through a doxycycline-inducible promoter system (Tet-On) integrated directly into the AAV-BHX-EpiReg vector. This system allows researchers to precisely manage the timing and duration of OSKM factor expression by administering low doses of doxycycline systemically in preclinical models.

## Initial Focus: Ocular Rejuvenation

The initial therapeutic target for Project Chronos Rewound is the reversal of age-related dysfunction in retinal ganglion cells (RGCs). The primary goal is to restore the axonal regeneration capacity lost with age and potentially develop treatments for debilitating conditions such as glaucoma and other age-related optic neuropathies.

## Safety Considerations and Partial Reprogramming

A critical aspect of the research is ensuring safety, particularly avoiding the induction of pluripotency or the formation of teratomas, which are known risks associated with sustained expression of reprogramming factors. The OSKM expression protocol developed under Project Chronos Rewound involves carefully calibrated, short pulses of induction (e.g., 48-72 hours) separated by longer periods where expression is turned off. This strategy aims to achieve partial epigenetic reprogramming – rejuvenating epigenetic marks associated with aging without completely erasing the cell's identity or triggering uncontrolled proliferation.

## Preclinical Validation and Advanced Monitoring

BioHarmonix conducts preclinical studies for Project Chronos Rewound primarily in aged non-human primate models, specifically rhesus macaques, which closely mimic human aging processes. Success is rigorously measured by quantifying improvements in visual evoked potentials (VEPs), assessing increased RGC survival rates following induced optic nerve injury, and demonstrating the reversal of specific age-associated DNA methylation patterns within the targeted retinal cells.

To monitor the reprogramming process *in vivo* and in real-time, the company is developing advanced imaging techniques. This involves using adaptive optics scanning laser ophthalmoscopy (AOSLO) combined with fluorescent reporters linked to the AAV-BHX-EpiReg vector, allowing direct visualization and assessment of cellular changes within the retina of research animals.

## Optimization and Future Directions

The research extends beyond the standard OSKM factors. Dr. Li's team is actively experimenting with alternative reprogramming factor combinations, such as substituting the oncogenic factor Myc with potentially safer alternatives like Glis1. They are also focused on optimizing the stoichiometric ratios of the factors delivered by the AAV vector to maximize rejuvenation efficacy while further minimizing safety risks.

Computational modeling plays a significant role in optimizing the protocols. Leveraging the AI resources of Marcus Thorne's QuantumLeap Capital, BioHarmonix uses sophisticated algorithms to predict optimal doxycycline dosing regimens and OSKM expression durations. These models aim to achieve desired levels of epigenetic age reversal, as measured by established epigenetic clocks like Horvath's clock, without inducing deleterious side effects in various cell types.

Ultimately, the successful demonstration of safe and effective epigenetic rejuvenation in ocular tissues through Project Chronos Rewound is viewed as a crucial stepping stone. It serves as a foundational proof-of-concept for BioHarmonix's broader ambition: developing systemic *in vivo* reprogramming therapies capable of targeting multiple organ systems, forming a key component of their envisioned 'Whole Body Systemic Rejuvenation' roadmap.
