# AI Ethics and Governance at Ember Automotive

Ember Automotive, co-founded by AI ethicist Dr. Samuel Green, places a strong emphasis on the ethical development and deployment of its artificial intelligence systems, particularly its advanced autonomous driving capabilities and the proprietary EmpathAI system. This commitment is operationalized through a robust governance structure and clearly defined principles, spearheaded by Dr. Green himself.

## The AI Ethics Review Board

Central to Ember's governance framework is the AI Ethics Review Board. This 7-member body includes Dr. Samuel Green as co-chair, Ember's Chief Technology Officer, Ember's Chief Legal Officer, two senior AI engineers (rotated annually), and two external academic ethicists specializing in machine learning and autonomous systems.

The Board convenes formally every quarter to address ongoing ethical considerations. Crucially, additional ad-hoc meetings are mandated before the deployment of any Level 3 or higher autonomous driving software update or any significant modification to the core inference models of the EmpathAI system. Demonstrating its authority, the AI Ethics Review Board holds veto power over the deployment of AI features. In a notable instance in 2024, the Board mandated significant modifications to the EmpathAI's fatigue detection algorithm after internal testing revealed statistically significant lower sensitivity for certain demographic groups. This decision delayed the feature's rollout by three months, underscoring the Board's commitment to fairness over expediency.

## Guiding Principles and Frameworks

Dr. Green's influence permeates Ember's ethical guidelines. He personally authored the company's internal 75-page 'AI Development & Deployment Ethics Handbook.' This comprehensive document includes mandatory pre-deployment checklists that cover critical areas such as fairness, bias detection (using specified metrics like demographic parity difference), privacy impact assessments, and explainability requirements.

A key principle is Dr. Green's 'explainable AI' mandate. This requires Ember's autonomous driving system to log not only its final decisions (like braking or steering) but also the confidence scores of alternative actions considered and the primary sensor data that influenced the chosen action. This level of detail is specifically designed for thorough post-incident analysis.

Furthermore, Ember adheres to a 'human-centric AI' philosophy under Dr. Green's guidance. The AI Ethics Review Board specifically evaluates new features for potential 'over-automation' risks. This ensures that drivers retain meaningful control over the vehicle and are not unduly influenced or deskilled by the AI systems designed to assist them.

## Accountability, Training, and Oversight

To ensure accountability, Ember Automotive maintains a 'Redress Request' system. This allows vehicle owners to flag specific autonomous driving behaviors or EmpathAI adjustments they find concerning. These requests are reviewed by the AI Ethics Review Board, which investigates potential systemic biases or failures indicated by user feedback.

Complementing this is an internal 'Bias Bounty Program,' overseen by the AI Ethics Review Board. This program encourages both Ember employees and vetted external security researchers to proactively identify and report potential biases within Ember's AI algorithms, offering financial rewards for validated findings.

The commitment to ethical AI extends to external components as well. Dr. Green's framework mandates that any third-party AI components or datasets integrated into Ember's systems must undergo the same rigorous ethical review process by the AI Ethics Review Board as internally developed systems, ensuring alignment with Ember's high standards.

Finally, ethical awareness is embedded in the engineering culture. All AI engineers at Ember are required to complete an annual 8-hour ethics refresher course. Designed by Dr. Green and the external board members, this training focuses on practical case studies derived from real-world autonomous vehicle incidents and explores ethical dilemmas relevant to affective computing, like that used in EmpathAI.

Through this multi-layered approach encompassing a dedicated review board, clear principles, robust accountability mechanisms, and continuous training, Ember Automotive strives to ensure its advanced AI systems are developed and deployed responsibly and ethically.
