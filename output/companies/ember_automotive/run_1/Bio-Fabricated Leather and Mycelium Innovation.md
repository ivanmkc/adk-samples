# Ember Automotive: Pioneering Bio-Fabricated Leather and Mycelium Innovation

Ember Automotive, known for its ultra-luxury electric vehicles and the guiding philosophy of 'Responsible Artistry,' continues to push the boundaries of sustainable materials. Beyond ethically sourced woods and textiles from recycled ocean plastics, the company is deeply invested in advanced bio-fabrication and mycelium technologies to redefine luxury interiors while minimizing environmental impact. Two key collaborations highlight this commitment: one with Modern Meadow for bio-fabricated leather and another with Ecovative Design for mycelium composites.

## Zoa™ Bioleather: Luxury Grown Through Biotechnology

Ember's pursuit of a sustainable alternative to traditional animal hides led to a partnership with Modern Meadow, initiated in 2020. This collaboration involved a dedicated joint research and development phase focused on customizing Modern Meadow's Zoa™ bioleather. The goal was to meet the exacting standards of Ember co-founder Isabella Rossi, particularly concerning the specific grain pattern and tactile softness required for the interiors of the Ember Aura and Solstice models.

The Zoa™ bioleather utilized by Ember represents a significant leap in materials science. It is derived from collagen proteins produced via a precision fermentation process. This process employs a genetically engineered strain of *Pichia pastoris* yeast, which is fed primarily with plant-derived sugars. This method eliminates the need for animal agriculture, aligning with Ember's ethical and sustainability goals.

Performance is paramount in automotive applications. Internal testing conducted by Ember confirmed that the customized Zoa™ bioleather meets or exceeds stringent automotive OEM standards for durability. Notably, it successfully endured over 100,000 cycles on the Wyzenbeek abrasion test and achieved high ratings (Grade 4-5 on ISO 105-B02) for UV resistance, ensuring longevity and resilience within the vehicle cabin.

Furthermore, the finishing process for Ember's Zoa™ bioleather prioritizes environmental responsibility. It utilizes bio-based tanning agents and dyes, significantly reducing the volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and heavy metal pollutants commonly associated with the traditional chrome tanning of animal hides. This commitment extends to understanding the full environmental picture; Ember has commissioned a lifecycle analysis (LCA), due in 2025, which will specifically compare the cradle-to-gate carbon footprint of Zoa™ bioleather against traditional bovine leather sourced from Leather Working Group (LWG) Gold-rated tanneries.

## Mycelium Composites: Growing the Future of Interior Structures

Building on its success with bio-fabricated leather, Ember Automotive embarked on another innovative materials venture in Q3 2022, establishing a formal research partnership with Ecovative Design, headquartered in Green Island, New York. This collaboration initially focuses on leveraging Ecovative's MycoComposite™ technology platform.

The primary goal is to develop lightweight yet durable interior structures using mycelium – the root structure of fungi. As of early 2024, the joint R&D team has achieved significant milestones. They have successfully prototyped mycelium-based dashboard substrates that demonstrate a targeted 30% weight reduction compared to conventional materials. Crucially, these prototypes have passed initial vibration and thermal cycling tests required for automotive interior components.

Ecovative's MycoComposite™ materials offer compelling sustainability advantages. They are grown using agricultural byproducts, such as hemp hurd, as feedstock. This process not only utilizes waste streams but also offers potential carbon sequestration benefits during the mycelium's growth phase. At the end of the vehicle's life, these mycelium components are fully biodegradable, contributing to a circular economy approach.

Ember has ambitious plans for integrating this technology. The company aims to introduce the first mycelium composite components, likely starting with door panel inserts, into its production vehicles. This introduction is tentatively planned as part of a mid-cycle refresh for the 2027 model year.

## Conclusion: Material Science Meets Responsible Artistry

Ember Automotive's collaborations with Modern Meadow and Ecovative Design underscore its commitment to 'Responsible Artistry.' By investing in and customizing advanced materials like Zoa™ bioleather and MycoComposite™ mycelium structures, Ember is actively demonstrating that cutting-edge technology, luxurious aesthetics, and profound environmental responsibility can coexist. These innovations are not merely replacements for traditional materials but represent a fundamental rethinking of how luxury vehicle interiors are conceived and manufactured for a sustainable future.
