# Atmospheric Reclaimers: Direct Air Capture and Sequestration at Site Boreas

TerraNova Geo-Engineering operates large-scale Direct Air Capture (DAC) facilities known as 'Atmospheric Reclaimers', designed to remove significant quantities of carbon dioxide directly from the ambient air. The flagship facility, codenamed 'Site Boreas', is strategically located in Iceland to leverage the region's abundant geothermal resources. This facility represents a major component of TerraNova's approach to climate intervention, aiming for large-scale carbon removal and permanent sequestration.

## Geothermal Power and Energy Requirements

A key feature of Site Boreas is its reliance on geothermal energy. The facility draws its primary power from the nearby 'Krafla Deep Heat' geothermal field. This involves utilizing superheated steam extracted from wells drilled over 2,500 meters deep. This direct use of geothermal heat is crucial for the energy-intensive DAC process.

The capture system employed at Site Boreas utilizes a liquid potassium hydroxide (KOH) solvent. Regenerating this solvent to release the captured CO2 requires temperatures exceeding 100°C. Site Boreas achieves this efficiently through direct geothermal heat exchange systems integrated with the power plant infrastructure. TerraNova estimates the specific energy consumption for the entire DAC process at Site Boreas – including both thermal and electrical energy – to be approximately 2,000 kWh per tonne of CO2 captured and processed.

## The Capture and Compression Process

Ambient air is processed through arrays of modular air contactors where the CO2 chemically binds to the KOH solvent. After capture, the CO2 is separated from the solvent during the regeneration phase. This process yields a highly concentrated CO2 stream, achieving a purity level exceeding 99.5%. This high purity is important for efficient downstream processing and sequestration, minimizing the risk of introducing contaminants into the geological storage formations.

Before injection, the captured CO2 undergoes significant compression, reaching pressures above 70 bar. This dense-phase CO2 is then mixed with large volumes of water, sourced from local glacial melt rivers, preparing it for injection underground.

## Sequestration via Mineralization

The primary method for long-term CO2 storage at Site Boreas is mineralization within deep basalt rock formations. The target formations are part of Iceland's Neogene flood basalts, specifically selected for their high porosity and chemical reactivity. These formations lie at depths between 800 and 2,000 meters beneath the site.

The mixture of compressed CO2 and water is injected into these basalt layers. Once underground, the dissolved CO2 reacts with minerals naturally present in the basalt, such as olivine and pyroxene. This geochemical reaction forms stable carbonate minerals, primarily calcite and magnesite, effectively locking the carbon into solid mineral form. TerraNova's models estimate that this mineralization process permanently sequesters over 95% of the injected CO2 within a timeframe of 2 to 5 years.

## Operational Scale and Monitoring

Site Boreas commenced initial pilot-scale operations in 2027. It reached its full target capture capacity of 1 million tonnes of CO2 per year in 2030, following a phased deployment of 40 modular DAC units. The entire facility, including the air contactor arrays, the central solvent regeneration plant, and the CO2 compression and injection infrastructure, occupies a physical footprint of approximately 50 hectares.

To ensure the effectiveness and safety of the sequestration process, TerraNova employs a sophisticated monitoring system. This includes a network of deep seismic sensors to track the subsurface CO2 plume and monitor for any induced seismicity. Additionally, periodic tracer gas analysis is conducted. Inert gases like sulfur hexafluoride (SF6) are injected alongside the CO2 stream, allowing scientists to track the movement and behavior of the injected fluids within the basalt formations and verify the progress of mineralization.

Site Boreas stands as a significant example of implementing DAC technology coupled with geological sequestration at scale, leveraging unique geological and energy resources to achieve substantial carbon dioxide removal.
