# Project Nimbus: Stratospheric Aerosol Injection Operations

Project Nimbus, a key initiative of TerraNova Geo-Engineering, focuses on experimental stratospheric aerosol injection (SAI) aimed at reflecting sunlight. The core operational activities for these experiments are centered around a specialized facility near Kiruna, Sweden.

## The StratoCruiser Drone Fleet

The project utilizes a fleet of bespoke high-altitude drones designated 'StratoCruisers'. These aircraft are engineered for extreme endurance and high-altitude flight. Key features include:

*   **Design:** A high-aspect-ratio wing design spanning over 50 meters, optimized for flight efficiency in the thin stratospheric air.
*   **Endurance:** Capable of sustained flights exceeding 72 hours at operational altitudes between 18 and 22 kilometers.
*   **Payload Capacity:** Each StratoCruiser can carry up to 2 tonnes of micronized calcium carbonate aerosol per mission.
*   **Fleet Size:** As of 2032, the operational fleet based near Kiruna comprises approximately 30 StratoCruiser drones, enabling near-continuous experimental deployment cycles.

## Aerosol Technology and Dispersal

Project Nimbus employs a carefully selected and engineered aerosol for its injection tests:

*   **Composition:** The primary material is synthetically produced vaterite polymorphs of calcium carbonate. This specific form is chosen and engineered for optimal particle size (0.5-1.5 micrometers) and surface properties.
*   **Objective:** The engineering aims to maximize solar reflectivity and increase the aerosol's atmospheric residence time within the stratosphere.
*   **Manufacturing and Logistics:** The specialized calcium carbonate is manufactured at a dedicated TerraNova facility in Norway. It is then transported to the Kiruna launch site via secure, climate-controlled rail shipments, overseen by TerraNova's 'Aegis' security division.
*   **Dispersal Mechanism:** Aerosol release is achieved using wing-mounted spray nozzles on the StratoCruisers. These nozzles are designed to create a controlled plume, optimized for dispersal within the specific atmospheric conditions encountered at altitudes between 18 and 22 kilometers.

## Ground Operations: Site Aurora

The launch, control, and support operations for Project Nimbus are managed from the TerraNova launch facility near Kiruna, codenamed 'Site Aurora'. This facility includes:

*   **Infrastructure:** Specialized hangars for the StratoCruiser fleet, dedicated aerosol storage and loading infrastructure.
*   **Mission Control:** A sophisticated mission control center staffed by approximately 150 personnel.
*   **Real-Time Monitoring:** During deployment flights, the StratoCruisers continuously transmit real-time data back to Site Aurora. This data includes aerosol plume dispersion characteristics, local ozone concentrations, stratospheric water vapor levels, and ambient temperature readings.
*   **Dynamic Control:** Flight paths and aerosol release schedules are not fixed but are dynamically adjusted in real-time by mission controllers. These adjustments utilize inputs from TerraNova's ClimateSim AI predictive models and live atmospheric sensor data relayed directly from the drones.

## Experimental Campaigns

Project Nimbus involves ongoing experimental campaigns to study the effects and behavior of the dispersed aerosols. A significant example was 'Operation Polar Veil', conducted throughout 2031. This campaign focused specifically on testing variable dispersal rates over the Arctic region during the polar winter to assess potential impacts on the polar vortex dynamics. The near-continuous operational capability provided by the StratoCruiser fleet allows for such sustained and targeted experimental phases.
